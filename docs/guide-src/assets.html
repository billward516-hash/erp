An asset is any piece of equipment you look after: a machine, a vehicle, a building space, a tool. Each has one record that collects its details, its documents, its open work, its maintenance plans and its track record. Start here when something is wrong with a machine, or when you want to know how a machine has behaved.

Who can do this: anyone with access to assets can search and read records. Technicians, maintenance managers, facilities managers and district managers can change a machine's state and record meter readings. Operators, technicians and most managers can report a problem. Maintenance managers and facilities managers can edit the repair-or-replace inputs and the spare-parts list. See Roles and access.

Find an asset

  1. Open Maintenance → Assets. The page is titled Equipment register.
  2. Type in Search. You can search by tag, name, model or serial number.
  3. Optionally pick a Current state to narrow the list.
  4. Click a row to open the asset.

The list shows site, tag, name, location, criticality, state and the number of open work orders. If you scan the QR code on a machine with your phone or tablet, it opens the same asset page directly.

What the states mean

An asset is always in one operational state. The state drives the downtime figures in the reports, so keep it honest.

StateMeaning
RunningMaking product or doing its job.
AvailableReady to use. A machine returns to this state when a work order is verified.
IdleWorking, but nothing is scheduled for it.
Planned downtimeStopped on purpose: maintenance, changeover, a holiday.
Unplanned downtimeStopped because something went wrong. This counts as a failure.
Out of serviceTaken out of use.

Change a machine's state

  1. Open the asset.
  2. Under Details, click Running, Idle, Down (unplanned) or Planned stop. The button for the current state is greyed out.

The page records when the state started. You cannot set Available or Out of service with these buttons; Available comes from verifying a work order (see Work orders).

Report a problem

  1. Open the asset and find the Report a problem box.
  2. Under What is wrong?, describe the symptom in a sentence or two (at least three characters).
  3. Pick a Severity: Low, Medium, High or Critical.
  4. Tick Machine is down if it has stopped.
  5. Click Send request.

This does not create a work order straight away. It sends a request to maintenance, who turn it into a work order or decline it. See Work orders: requests.

What is on an asset page

  • Details: class, manufacturer and model, serial number, criticality, in-service date, current state and how long it has been in it, plus any custom fields your company added.
  • Change banner: if a change request affects this asset, a yellow bar names it, with a link. Temporary changes show their end date. See Changes and notices.
  • Qualification and logbook: for people who may see them, the tool's qualification state and a link to its qualification page, protocols and equipment logbook. See Equipment qualification.
  • Technical documents: approved manuals and procedures linked to the asset, with an Open link. A document with no approved version shows No approved version. See Document control.
  • Reliability: described below.
  • Preventive maintenance: the plans for this asset, and a place to record meter readings (only if the PM feature is on).
  • Spare parts: the parts that belong on this machine and how many you have at the site.
  • Repair templates: stock repair jobs with their labor hours and parts.
  • Open work orders and Components: the child assets that belong to this one.

Record a meter reading

Meters (run hours, cycles, miles) trigger meter-based preventive maintenance.

  1. On the asset page, go to Preventive maintenance.
  2. Type or pick the Meter name. If the asset has a meter plan, its meter name is suggested.
  3. Enter the Reading (the total on the meter, not the change since last time).
  4. Click Record meter reading. A message tells you how much is left before the next plan is due.

Reliability

The Reliability card shows failures, mean time between failures (MTBF), mean time to repair (MTTR) and availability for the last 30, 90 or 365 days, with a month-by-month table. A failure is an unplanned stop. MTBF is uptime divided by failures. MTTR is unplanned downtime divided by failures.

A note under the figures says where the numbers came from: from state history (best), or estimated from work orders when nobody recorded states, or no data. If state records cover only part of the period, the page tells you how much. Failure codes that repeat within 30 days appear in red.

Good reliability numbers depend on people pressing the state buttons and on technicians completing work orders with a failure code. A machine with no state history still gets estimates, but they are rougher.

Repair or replace

For machines and vehicles (not components or spaces), the Repair or replace card compares the yearly cost of keeping the asset (maintenance plus the cost of unplanned downtime, for this asset and its components) with the equivalent yearly cost of a new one. It shows one of three verdicts: Keep, Replace or Not enough data, with the reason in a sentence, the year replacing starts to pay, and a table by year.

  1. Click Edit the inputs.
  2. Enter the Replacement cost, the Expected life (years) and the Downtime cost / h. Without a downtime cost, the downtime column shows a dash and is left out of the comparison.
  3. Click Save.

This is a guide, not a decision. It uses full years only, and it leaves out resale value, energy, safety and parts availability. Use it to start the conversation about capital spending.

Common mistakes

  • Reporting a problem and expecting a work order. A request only becomes a work order when a maintenance planner accepts it.
  • Leaving a machine in Unplanned downtime after it is fixed. Verifying the work order does this for you; if you fixed it without a work order, press Running.
  • Entering a meter reading as the amount since last time. Enter the reading on the meter.

Administrators: people who may manage assets get a New asset button on this page (site, tag, name, kind, criticality, make, model and serial number; the rest is added on the asset page). Large numbers of assets are better loaded by import. See Importing data and Sites and people. The PM card and meter box only appear when the preventive maintenance feature is on; see Features and editions.
