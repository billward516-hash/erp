Document control makes sure the people doing a job use the right version of its instructions. Procedures (SOPs), work instructions, test methods, forms, drawings, manuals, customer specifications and standards live in one repository. Each revision is written by one person, approved by another, and takes effect on a stated date. Old revisions are kept but marked as replaced, and every time someone opens a document it is logged. This is the "documented information" an auditor asks for under ISO 9001 clause 7.5.

Who can do this:

  • Read approved documents: almost everyone ("view documents"). Restricted documents need the extra "view restricted documents" permission, and a folder or document can be limited to named roles.
  • See drafts: people with "view draft documents", such as technicians, managers and quality staff.
  • Create documents, upload revisions, send for review, link, make folders, run the audit pack: people with "manage documents", for example maintenance managers, quality managers and facilities managers.
  • Approve a revision or make a document obsolete: people with "approve documents" at the site, such as maintenance managers and administrators in the starter roles. Your company may have given the permission to other roles.

The repository

Open Quality & safety → Document control. A sentence at the top summarises the state. The tiles show controlled documents, external ones (customer specifications and standards), those in review, those waiting for their effective date, and those waiting on you. Use the search box (number, title, issuer's number, or text), and filters for kind, origin (internal or external), folder and status. A row without a revision in force is highlighted. The Next column shows a coming revision or one in review. People who may view reports also see a link to the master list report.

Kinds of document

Standard operating procedure, work instruction, procedure, method (test or inspection), technical requirement, customer specification, standard, form or template, drawing, manual, troubleshooting guide, repair procedure, service bulletin, training material, photo, video, contract and other. Customer specifications and standards are external: we control our copy and where it applies, but someone else owns the content. All others are internal. If you don't give a number, one is assigned from a prefix per kind (for example SOP-, WI-, TM-).

Create a document

  1. Click New document.
  2. Choose the Kind, optionally a number, the Site (or company-wide) and Folder, and enter the title and a purpose and scope.
  3. For an external kind, enter who issued it, their number and revision, and the date you received it.
  4. Upload the First version file and give your Our revision label (for example A). Or, for a procedure, work instruction or method, tick Write the steps here in the app instead of uploading a file (see below).
  5. Tick Restricted readers only or Safety-critical if they apply, then click Create document.

The first version is a draft. Nobody else can use it until it is approved. PDFs, images and text open in the browser; other file types are downloaded. Companies moving from another system can load many files at once with Bring in files from another system (a zip with an index sheet; it is checked first and nothing loads until it is clean).

What the statuses mean

StatusMeaning
DraftUploaded, not yet sent for review. Not approved for use.
In reviewWaiting for reviewers and the approver to sign.
Approved, effective on a dateApproved, but not yet in force. The previous revision still applies until the date.
In forceThe revision everyone must use.
SupersededReplaced by a newer revision. Don't work to it.
ObsoleteThe document is withdrawn, with a reason. Don't use it.

Whenever you open a revision that is not the one in force, a red banner says so, with a button to show the revision in force.

Revise, review and approve

  1. Open the document. Under Control choose New revision: upload the file, give a label, and say What changed and why. The reason is required for every revision after the first. Only one draft can exist at a time. Click Upload as a draft.
  2. Choose reviewers if you want them (up to eight; they sign first) and the Approver. The approver must be someone else than the author and must hold approval rights at the site.
  3. Set Effective from, or leave it empty to put the revision in force as soon as it is approved. Choose a later date to train people first. It cannot be in the past.
  4. Click Send for review. Reviewers and the approver get the request in Home and approvals. Each must add a comment with their decision; the comment is the meaning of their signature.
  5. When the approver approves, the revision is released. A released revision's content can never change; corrections need a new revision.

Someone with approval rights who isn't the author can also use Approve now (one step) from a draft. The author can never approve their own revision. To pull a revision back, use Withdraw from review with a reason, or Discard the draft. To retire a document, an approver clicks Make obsolete with a reason of at least five characters, once no revision is in review.

You can compare any two revisions line by line under Compare revisions: added lines show with + and removed lines with −.

Work instructions written in the app

For a procedure, work instruction, SOP or method you can write the steps in the app instead of uploading a file. Click Write the steps in the app (or Edit the steps of revision X). Fill in the purpose, scope, hazards, protective equipment and tools (one per line), then add numbered steps, each with optional caution, a check or measurement with its specification, and photos. Move steps up or down. Click Save as a draft and send it for review like any other revision. Hazards show first when someone reads it.

Reading and printing

A document opens in the viewer showing the revision in force. A notice reminds you that online copies are controlled and printed copies are not. A printout is stamped "UNCONTROLLED WHEN PRINTED" with the number, revision and print date. Check the revision on screen before you rely on paper. Every opening and printing is logged with who and when. If a temporary change is in force on the document, a banner shows it above the text (see Temporary SOP changes).

Read and understood

A manager can require everyone in a group to read and acknowledge each new revision. Under Read and understood click Change, tick Require every revision to be read and acknowledged and choose roles (none ticked means everyone who may read it at that site).

  1. When the revision is released, each person in the audience gets an obligation. It is due on the revision's effective date, or a set number of days after release (7 unless your company changed it).
  2. Open the document. A banner says Read and acknowledge this revision by …. You must open and read the revision first.
  3. Click I have read and understood this revision.

The acknowledgement is a permanent record. If a newer revision is released before you acknowledge, the old obligation is replaced by the new one. Managers see per revision how many must read, how many have, and how many are overdue, and can open Who has not read it.

Customer specifications and standards

An external document records the issuer, their number and their revision (and the date received) for each of our copies. When a new revision of an external document arrives, the system starts an impact review: someone must decide, within a set number of working days, whether it affects our parts, processes or documents. The new revision cannot be put in force until the review is decided.

  1. Open the document. Under Revision impact reviews read the due date.
  2. Choose No impact on us or Changes required. Write what changed and your assessment.
  3. For changes required, enter the customer's implementation date and tick our documents that must be revised. Choose an owner and due date for each. Each follow-up closes itself when that document releases its next revision.
  4. Click Record the review.

Use Where it applies to link the parts and customers an external document covers, and to link our own documents as "works to" the specification. The repository also lists specification revisions waiting for review and the follow-ups due.

Electronic signatures

For the kinds of document your administrator has chosen, reviewers and approvers must sign electronically. They re-enter their sign-in credential at the moment of signing. The signature records who, what it means (reviewed or approved), when, and a checksum of the exact file signed, sealed so it cannot be altered. Signatures appear under Electronic signatures on the document. The approve-now button asks for the credential too.

The audit pack

Click Audit pack on the repository page to download a zip an auditor can be handed. It holds the master list and obsolete register, every revision with its author, reviewers, approver, reason and effective date, each signature (rechecked to show it is intact), every acknowledgement, the specification reviews, the log of views and changes, the released files, and a manifest of checksums that says whether the audit trail verifies. Each document also has its own audit pack link under Control. Only documents you manage are included.

Folders

Managers use New folder to organise documents, optionally inside another folder, and to limit reading to certain roles. With no roles ticked, everyone may read.

Common mistakes

  • Printing a procedure and using it for months. Printed copies are uncontrolled. Check the screen.
  • Choosing yourself as the approver. The system refuses; the approver must be someone else.
  • Setting the effective date to today, then training afterwards. Choose a later date so people can be trained first.
  • Treating a banner as optional. A temporary-change banner is part of the procedure while it is in force.

Administrators choose, under Document settings (only visible to administrators on this page): the working days allowed for a specification review, the days after release a read-and-understood is due, the longest temporary SOP change in days, and which kinds of document require electronic signatures. Document access can also be limited by role and site. For people who sign in through a company identity provider, re-entering a credential depends on that provider's setup.
