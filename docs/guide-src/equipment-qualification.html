Some equipment has to be qualified before it is used for production: someone installs it, tests that it works, and shows it performs, and a second person signs off the evidence. After a repair or a change, the tool has to be shown to still work. This page covers how the platform records that. It keeps the protocols and their results, refuses to let a tool that needs requalification return to service, reminds you when requalification is due, and keeps an equipment logbook and a report of how each tool's time was spent.

The platform keeps the records; your company owns its qualification approach and the validation of its own processes. Nothing here certifies a tool, a process or your company against any standard.

Who can do this: Equipment qualification is part of the Standard edition and is not in Lite. Reading (the list, protocols, the logbook, the reports) needs the qualification.view permission: maintenance managers, plant managers, quality managers and reviewers, EHS reviewers, facilities managers, technicians and executives hold it. Putting tools under control, writing protocols and setting intervals needs qualification.manage (maintenance managers and quality managers). Recording results, executing a protocol and adding logbook notes needs qualification.execute (maintenance managers and technicians). Approval is not a permission: it goes to the person named on the protocol, or to anyone holding the Quality manager role at the site. See the Permissions table.

What a tool's qualification can be

Only tools you put under qualification control have a state. For every other tool nothing changes.

StateMeaningCan it come out of downtime?
QualifiedAn approved PQ or requalification (or a recorded existing qualification) stands, and no work or change has happened since.Yes
Requalification dueQualified, and its requalification date is within the warning period (30 days unless your company changed it) or has passed. The page shows the date and how many days remain or are overdue.Yes
Requalification requiredWork or a change was done on the tool, or a protocol on it failed. It stays required until a passing protocol executed after that date is approved.No
Not qualifiedUnder control, but no PQ or requalification has been approved yet and no existing qualification has been recorded.No

Colour is never the only cue: each state is written on the page.

Put a tool under qualification control

  1. Open Maintenance → Qualification. The page is titled Equipment qualification.
  2. Under Put a tool under qualification control, choose the Tool.
  3. Leave Requalify after finished work or an implemented change ticked unless the tool should not need requalification after every job (see below). It is ticked by default.
  4. Click Put under control. The tool's own qualification page opens. You can also do this from that page, which is also reached from Open qualification and logbook on the asset page.

A new tool starts as Not qualified. It becomes qualified when a PQ or requalification protocol is approved with every step passed.

A tool that was qualified before it was entered here

  1. On the tool's qualification page, click Record existing qualification.
  2. Enter the date it was qualified, an optional requalification due date, and the Basis: which earlier protocol, report or document shows it. At least ten characters are required.
  3. Click Record.

The basis is kept in the tool's history. This works only for a tool that has never been qualified here. It cannot clear Requalification required; only an approved protocol does that.

To take a tool out of qualification control, click Switch qualification control off for this tool and give a reason. Nothing is gated for it afterwards, and the reason stays in the history.

What makes a qualified tool need requalification

  • A work order on the tool is completed or verified (any kind of work order, preventive ones included).
  • A change that lists the tool among the things it affects goes in service.
  • Either of those happens on a component of the tool (a child asset): the tool above it is marked.
  • A requalification, OQ, PQ or IQ protocol on the tool is approved as failed.

The same work order or change marks a tool once. If the tool is already marked, it keeps its earlier date. A tool that is not yet qualified is not marked. The tick box Requalify after work on the tool's qualification page turns the work and change triggers off for that tool; it does not turn off the failed-protocol rule. Managers who hold qualification management at the site are notified when a tool becomes required.

Return to service is refused

For a tool under control that is Requalification required or Not qualified, these are refused with a message that names the tool and the reason:

  • Changing its state from planned downtime, unplanned downtime or out of service to available, running or idle, from the asset page, the qualification page or the API.
  • Verifying a work order with Verify and return to service, when the tool is down. The work order stays completed.

Moving from one downtime state to another, and stating the state of a tool that is already running, are not affected. Imported state history is not a return to service.

To close the paperwork without returning the tool to service, use the button Verify, keep out of service that appears on the work order after the refusal. The tool stays down. When its requalification is approved, set its state to available yourself.

Write and run a protocol

A protocol belongs to one tool. It is one of: IQ (installation qualification), OQ (operational qualification), PQ (performance qualification) or requalification.

  1. On the tool's qualification page, click New protocol.
  2. Choose the Type, give it a Title, and optionally write the Acceptance criteria, a Requalification interval (months) and a named Approver. Without a named approver, anyone holding the Quality manager role at the site can approve it.
  3. Type the steps in the box, one per line, as the step | what is expected. Click Create draft.
  4. On the protocol page, link the controlled documents it follows (below), add steps with Add step, and attach files.
  5. Do the work. For each step click Record result, choose Pass, Fail or Not applicable, write What was found (the actual result), and click Save result. Photos, instrument printouts and certificates can be added under Evidence for this step, or to the protocol as a whole under Files.
  6. When every step has a result, write the Conclusion and click Execute and send for approval. The results are now locked.
  7. The approver gets the protocol in Approvals as Approval of QP-0001 (OQ) and must write a comment saying what they checked. Approving a protocol in which every step passed makes it Approved.

Who may approve

The approver must be someone other than the person who executed the protocol and other than anyone who recorded a result in it. The platform refuses a named approver who took part when the protocol is executed, and refuses an approval from such a person even if they hold the Quality manager role. The approver who is a delegate of someone who took part is refused too.

These electronic records do not use the optional electronic signatures of document control, which belong to controlled documents. The approval is recorded with the approver's name, the time and their comment, and the history cannot be edited.

Protocol statuses

StatusMeaning
DraftBeing written; results can be recorded. Only a draft can be edited or cancelled.
Executed, waiting for approvalEvery step has a result and the protocol is with the approver.
ApprovedThe approver accepted a run in which every step passed (steps marked not applicable do not count against it).
FailedThe approver reviewed a run in which one or more steps failed and it is recorded as failed. Write a new protocol once the cause is dealt with.

If the approver rejects the protocol, with a comment saying why, it goes back to Draft: the reason is in the tool's history, results can be corrected, and it can be executed again.

A protocol executed before work was done does not count for it, even if it is approved afterwards; the history says why. After a clearing approval the tool's qualification date is today, and the next requalification date is the approval date plus the protocol's interval.

Controlled documents

On a draft protocol, search for a document under Find a document, choose it, pick whether it is the Procedure or a Reference, and click Link document. Only documents with an approved version can be linked. The link is pinned to the version in force when you linked it. If the document is revised later, the protocol shows Revised since linked beside the two versions, so you can see which one the work followed. See Document control.

Requalification dates and intervals

Each protocol type has a default interval, shown and changed under Requalification intervals on the Qualification page: IQ none; OQ, PQ and requalification 12 months. A protocol can use its own. When a protocol with an interval is approved as passed, the tool's requalification due date is set to the approval date plus the interval. Leave an interval empty for no periodic requalification.

  • The Qualification list shows each tool's date and how many days remain.
  • A date that has passed appears on What is late under Equipment requalification overdue. An overdue date alone does not stop a tool returning to service; Requalification required does.
  • The daily reminder Equipment requalification due sends the people who manage qualification at the site one summary a week while any tool is due within the warning period or overdue. See Notifications and reminders.

The equipment logbook

The logbook is one list for a tool, on its qualification page: state changes, work orders (opened, completed, verified), calibrations, qualification events (switched on, protocols executed, approved, failed or returned, requalification required and cleared), changes that affect it (raised, authorized, put in service, closed) and manual notes. Entries are read from the records that own them, so the logbook cannot disagree with them. Use Show to narrow it to one kind and Order for newest or oldest first.

  • Notes. Type under Add a note to the logbook and click Add note. Who wrote it and when are kept. A note cannot be edited or removed; to correct one, add another.
  • State changes. Use Record a state change. For an available, running or idle state you can tick Engineering use (trials, process work, qualification runs).
  • Export. The PDF, CSV and Excel buttons export the logbook as you filtered it, the same way other reports export, and the export is recorded in the audit trail.

If you may not see a kind of record (for example calibrations), the logbook leaves it out and says so above the table, and again in the export. Treat such a logbook as incomplete.

The tool states report

Open Reports → Tool states (SEMI E10 style), or Tool states report on the Qualification page. For each tool and period it shows hours and the share of the whole period in six groups. The platform's six states are mapped to the groups like this:

GroupMade from
ProductiveRunning
StandbyAvailable and idle
EngineeringAvailable, running or idle that was recorded with Engineering use
Scheduled downtimePlanned downtime
Unscheduled downtimeUnplanned downtime
Non-scheduledOut of service

Time with no state record is shown as Not recorded and is not counted in any group, so a gap in the records is never taken for production. The six groups and Not recorded add up to the whole period. A state that is still open counts up to now.

This is a reading of the state history in the manner of SEMI E10; it is not a certified implementation of the standard. Confirm the mapping suits your own definitions before you quote the figures. See Features and editions to turn the feature on or off.

The other report, Equipment qualification status, lists every tool under control with its state, dates, the reason requalification is required and its open protocols.

Common mistakes

  • Executing a protocol before the work is finished. A protocol must be executed after the date requalification became required to clear it. Do the repair, then run the protocol.
  • Naming yourself, or someone who ran the test, as approver. The platform refuses it. Name someone independent, or leave the approver blank for the Quality manager role.
  • Using an IQ after a repair. An IQ does not clear requalification. Use an OQ, PQ or requalification protocol.
  • Expecting the tool to return to service on its own. Approving the protocol clears the requirement; someone still sets the tool's state back to available or running.
  • Putting every tool under control. Every finished job on a controlled tool makes it need requalification unless Requalify after work is off. Choose the tools where that is what you mean.

What happens next

  • When a tool becomes Requalification required, the people who manage qualification at the site get a notice.
  • When a protocol is executed, its approver gets an approval task; when it is approved or rejected, the person who executed it is told.
  • Every step, result, approval and state change is recorded with who and when. Executed results and the qualification history cannot be changed.

Not included: connections to measuring instruments or manufacturing equipment, wafer or lot tracking, and any claim of compliance with a regulation. If you need to prove your records to an auditor, the logbook, the protocols and the audit trail are the evidence the platform keeps.
