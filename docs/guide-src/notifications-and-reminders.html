Notifications are the system's way of tapping you on the shoulder: something is waiting for you, a request of yours was decided, or something you own is overdue. This page explains where they appear, what creates them, why you may not get the same one twice, and how to turn kinds off.

Where you see them

  • The bell in the top bar shows a count of unread notifications. Click it to open the list.
  • My day → Notifications (title For you) lists them, newest first, up to the latest 50.
  • Email, once a day, if you have unread items and email is switched on for you (see the daily summary).

Using the list

  1. Open Notifications. Unread items have a dot beside the headline.
  2. Click an item. It is marked read and you go straight to the record it is about (a work order, an approval, a timesheet).
  3. To tidy up without opening each one, choose Mark all read.
  4. To see only what you have not dealt with, tick Only unread.

When the list is empty it says “Nothing here. You are all caught up.” Marking a notification read does not complete the task behind it; the work order is still late, the approval still waits. For that, use Home and approvals.

What creates notifications

There are two sorts. Events happen at once when something is done to a record that involves you. Reminders are produced by a daily check that looks for things that are due, late or expiring. Most of the common kinds can be muted under Settings on the Notifications page (“Notify me when…”); a few, such as a published policy to read or a performance review notice, are not listed there and always arrive.

Reminders

A daily check creates reminders such as:

  • Your work orders are overdue; PM plans are due with no work order (planners).
  • Tools are due for requalification or past their date (people who manage equipment qualification).
  • Your training is due or overdue; your qualifications are expiring.
  • You have not submitted last week's timesheet; timesheets are waiting for your approval.
  • Corrective and safety actions are overdue; corrective-action verifications are due; calibrations are due.
  • Purchase orders are late (buyers); supplier invoices wait for approval; customer invoices are overdue; sales and collections follow-ups are due; a quote you sent expires soon (one notice at 7 days, one at 2 days, and one the day after, not more).
  • Chemical containers or prepared reagents are reaching their use-by date, or stored chemicals have no current safety data sheet (chemical managers; see Chemicals and reagents).
  • Condition readings were missed; leases end without renewal; kits are short of parts for work due soon.
  • A record is due for its periodic review, or a performance review is due or overdue.

Your administrator decides which reminders are on, how often they repeat, and who is told. See Administrator brief: reminders and retention.

Why you do not get the same one twice

Reminders carry a memory. A reminder about the same item for the same person is created once per period (your administrator sets it per reminder: daily, weekly, or just once), not every time the daily check runs. This keeps the bell useful. If a reminder reached you and you have not fixed the cause, the next one comes when the period is up. Opening or reading a notification does not trigger a new one; fixing the cause (submitting the timesheet, completing the work order) stops them.

Choosing what you are told about

  1. Open Notifications and scroll to Settings.
  2. Under Notify me when…, untick the kinds you do not want. The change saves at once.
  3. Tick it again to turn it back on.

Muting a kind stops new notifications of that kind being created for you; they are not hidden after the fact. Do not mute Something is waiting for my sign-off if you approve things for other people, or you will not know they are waiting.

The daily email summary

If you tick Email me a daily summary of unread items (on by default), the system builds one email per day with up to 50 of your unread notifications that have not been emailed before, each with a link. It is sent to the email address on your user record, shown beside the checkbox. If you have nothing unread, or you already read everything, no email is sent. Items that were emailed are not included again.

The daily summary and the reminder check run in the nightly scheduled job (Admin → Scheduled jobs). Email is delivered only when the system has a mail server set up (the Email card on the Scheduled jobs page says whether it has, and Send a test email to me checks it). Until then summaries are prepared but not delivered to inboxes. The delivery code has been tested only against a stand-in mail server, not yet against a real provider, so confirm with the test email before telling people to expect it. A message that fails is kept and tried again by the next daily run. Check this with whoever set up your system. See Administrator brief: scheduled jobs and reminders and retention.

Common mistakes

  • Waiting for an email that never comes. Check that the summary is on, that your email address is on your user record, and that the system has a mail server set up (an administrator can check on the Scheduled jobs page). If it has not, the summary is prepared but not delivered; the bell still has everything. Your summary is not sent if you turned it off after it was prepared.
  • Treating “read” as “done”. Marking all read clears the count, not the task.
  • Expecting history. The list shows your latest items; old ones are tidied away by your company's retention rules.
