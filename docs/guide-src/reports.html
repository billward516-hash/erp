Reports turn your records into answers. Each report opens with a headline sentence that states its main finding, then shows the key numbers, a few supporting points, charts and the full table behind them. The same figures appear on screen and in every export, for the same filters. Open Insight → Reports.

Who can do this:

  • Open reports: most roles have the right to run reports (permission "view reports"). Each report also needs the right to see the underlying records. For example, the Accounts payable aging report needs the right to view payables, and a business case report needs the right to view business cases. If you open one you lack the right for, the system tells you.
  • Export to CSV, Excel and PDF, and share a saved view: managers, buyers, finance and sales staff, bookkeepers and executives (permission "export reports"). The export buttons do not appear for people without it.
  • Delete a saved view: its owner, or an administrator.

Choose a report

The Reports page lists the reports available to your company as cards with a one-line description. Click one to run it. The list is shorter if your edition or switched-off features exclude some. In the Lite edition you get Profit and loss, Balance sheet, Accounts receivable aging, Accounts payable aging and the expense summary.

Read a report

  1. The headline. The large sentence at the top is the report's finding, such as "Net income $42,000 on $310,000 of income (13.5% margin)" or "No open customer invoices in scope". Read it first; the rest supports it.
  2. The line under it shows the period (if the report has one), the scope (which sites the numbers cover) and when it was generated, in UTC.
  3. The tiles are the key numbers. A tile's color shows its status: green is good, amber needs a look, red is bad, no color is neutral. A small note gives context such as a target.
  4. The supporting points list three to five facts behind the headline.
  5. The charts show the same numbers visually. If your personal setting prefers tables, charts are folded into a Charts section you can open.
  6. The table has every row. Rows with a red edge need attention. A dash means no data is available, which is not the same as zero.
  7. The notes under the table explain definitions and data limits, and name the source.

When a row links to a source record, an Open link at its right takes you to it.

Tables with eight or more rows have a search box and sorting for the rows already loaded.

Filter a report

  1. In Filters and saved views, set the filters the report offers. They differ by report; common ones are Site (default All my sites), From, To, As of, Days back and Fiscal year.
  2. Click Apply. The page and the address both change, so you can bookmark or share the link.
  3. Click Reset to go back to the defaults.

You only ever see data for the sites you are allowed to see. Two people running the same report can get different numbers because their scope differs.

Save a view

  1. Set the filters you use often and click Apply.
  2. Under Name these filters, type a name (at least two letters).
  3. Tick Share with the company if you want others to use it. This tickbox appears only for people who can export reports.
  4. Click Save view.

Choose a view from Saved views to apply it. Saving under an existing name of your own replaces it. Delete view appears next to the view you have selected if it is yours (or you are an administrator).

Export and print

If you can export, three buttons sit at the top right of the report.

ButtonWhat you get
CSVA plain table that opens in any spreadsheet. It begins with a few lines describing the report, finding, period, scope and filters, so a forwarded file explains itself. Percentages are written as ratios from 0 to 1. Blank cells mean no data, not zero.
ExcelA workbook with the same figures.
PDFA formatted document with the tiles, charts and table. Use this to print or send by email.

Each export uses the filters currently applied and is recorded in the audit trail with the filters and the number of rows. To print, download the PDF and print that.

Reports and "what is late"

Reports describe a period or a point in time and can be exported. What is late is a live list of items already past their date. Use it for daily follow-up and reports for analysis.

Common mistakes

  • Reading a dash as zero. A dash means the figure could not be worked out from the data.
  • Comparing two people's numbers. Check the scope line; different sites give different totals.
  • Forgetting the period. Check From and To before you export for someone else.

Which reports appear follows the edition and the feature switches; the role that lets someone export reports is set in role settings. See Features and editions and Permissions table.
