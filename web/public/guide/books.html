    Books

    The built-in books: journal, bank lines, reconciliation, period close and financial statements.

    On this page

    The Books are a small-business set of accounts built into the platform. Customer invoices, supplier invoices, expense reports and payments post to them as they happen. You bring in your bank and card statements, match or categorize each line, reconcile to the statement, and read your profit and loss and balance sheet. Open Finance → Books.

    The built-in books are in both the Lite and Standard editions. They are an alternative to an external accounting system, not an addition to one: a company uses either the built-in books or a connected accounting system. They do not cover payroll processing, tax filing, multiple currencies, consolidation, fixed-asset depreciation schedules or inventory valuation.

    • Read the overview, accounts, journal and statements: finance approvers, executives, bookkeepers and administrators (permission "view books").
    • Set up the books, categorize and match bank lines, add accounts, post journal entries, reconcile, close a period: bookkeepers and administrators (permission "manage books").

    Set up the books

    The first time, if the books have not been set up, a Set up the books form appears. Only someone who can manage the books sees the form; others see a note that setup has not happened.

    1. Enter your main bank account's name (it starts as Checking) and, if you like, its last four digits.
    2. Set Balance on to the date you want the books to start, and type the Opening balance the account had that day.
    3. Click Set up the books.

    This creates a starter chart of accounts, the bank account, and starts posting invoices, bills, expense reports and payments from then on. What customers still owe on invoices already issued, and what you owe on approved bills and expense reports, comes in as opening balances. Balances from a previous system come in from its trial balance (see below).

    If an external accounting connection is active, the setup page tells you to disconnect it under Accounting first. Only one accounting target is active at a time. See Accounting connection.

    TabWhat it shows

    Bank and card lines

    Bring in a statement

    1. Click Import a statement on the Banking tab. You go to Data import.
    2. Choose Bank and card transactions and upload your bank's CSV file. The importer reads the bank's own layout. See Importing data.

    Match or categorize each line

    Show Unreviewed lines (the default) and work down the list. For each line you have these choices:

    • Match: if a payment you already recorded in the platform fits the amount and date, a button starting Match and the journal entry number appears. Click it to tie the bank line to that payment, so nothing is counted twice.
    • Categorize: choose an Account (a rule may have suggested one) and click Categorize. Tick Rule first to remember it: later lines whose description contains the same words get the account suggested.
    • Exclude: for lines that are not business transactions or are already recorded.

    Use the Show box to look at Matched, Categorized and Excluded lines later. The Rules table at the bottom lists the rules you have saved.

    Journal

    The journal records every posting as a balanced entry: total debits equal total credits. Entries are never edited; a correction is a reversal, shown with a Reversal or Reversed badge. The From column shows what created the entry: an invoice, bill, expense report, payment, bank line or a manual entry.

    Post a manual entry

    Use this for things that do not come from an invoice, bill, expense report or bank line: an owner contribution, a loan, a depreciation charge or a correction.

    1. On the Journal tab set the Date and a Memo saying what it is for.
    2. For each line choose an Account and enter a Debit or a Credit. Click Add line if you need more than two.
    3. Watch the line under the lines: it turns green when debits equal credits.
    4. Click Post entry.

    Chart of accounts

    The starter chart can be added to. Click Add an account at the bottom: give it a code, name and type (asset, liability, equity, income or expense). For an asset you can say whether it is a bank, current asset, fixed asset or inventory account. System accounts are marked (system). Click any account in the list to see its register from the start of the year with opening and closing balances.

    Statements

    The Statements tab shows three statements for the period you choose, plus the trial balance: the Profit and loss for the range, the Balance sheet as of the end date and the Cash flow for the range. The trial balance carries a Balanced or Out of balance badge.

    1. Pick a period with the buttons (This month, Last month, This quarter, Year to date, Last year) or set From and To dates.
    2. Choose what to Show: This period only; Against the prior period (the same number of days just before); Against the same period last year; Month by month (one column per month, with a total); or Site by site (one column per site, a No site column for entries posted without one, and a total). A comparison and a split cannot be combined.
    3. Optionally choose one Site to see only the entries posted to it.
    4. Click Statements pack (PDF) or Statements pack (Excel) for all three statements in one file, headed with the company name and period and marked as unaudited management statements.

    How to read them

    • Comparisons. The balance sheet compares with the end of the previous month (or the same date last year). Net income shows the change and the percentage change against the comparison column.
    • Cash flow. It starts from net income and adjusts for the change in every other account: receivables, stock, payables and so on under operating activities, equipment purchases under investing, and loans and owner money under financing. Accumulated depreciation is recognized by its name. The statement must tie to the change in your bank accounts, and a badge says whether it does; if it does not, the difference is shown so you can look for a mis-typed account type.
    • By site. Only entries posted with a site count towards that site. Bank, tax and other company-wide lines usually have no site, so a balance sheet or cash flow for one site will not balance or tie by itself; the page says so. The profit and loss by site always adds up to the company because the No site column catches the rest.
    • Basis. Accrual: invoices count when issued, bills when approved, expense reports when approved.

    These are management statements made from the books you keep here. They are not audited or reviewed, and the platform does not file taxes or prepare year-end accounts. Give them to your accountant as a starting point.

    • Click Export journal (CSV) to download every entry in the range. This is also the file to hand to a new accounting system if you move.
    • Click Print or export the reports to open the Profit and loss report, where you can export Excel or PDF and print. The Balance sheet and Cash flow are also in Reports.

    Reconcile to a statement

    1. Open the Reconcile tab.
    2. Choose the bank or card Account, set the Statement date and type the Ending balance from the statement. Click Start.
    3. Tick Cleared beside each deposit and payment that appears on the statement.
    4. Watch the three tiles: Statement balance, Cleared in the books and Difference. The difference turns green at zero.
    5. When the difference is zero, click Finish reconciliation. It moves to the Finished list.

    Only one reconciliation is open at a time. You cannot finish while the difference is not zero.

    Close a period

    1. Open Settings, find Close a period.
    2. Set Closed through to the last day you want locked, such as the end of the year.
    3. Click Save.

    Nothing can be posted on or before that date. Invoices, bills, expenses and bank lines dated earlier are refused with a message. Clear the date to unlock. Your accountant usually asks for this after year end.

    Starting partway through the year

    On Settings, Bring in open items posts opening balances for open customer invoices, approved bills and approved expense reports recorded before the books started. You can run it again; nothing posts twice. Import a trial balance opens Data import for your old system's balances in its own layout.

    What the signs mean

    What you seeMeaning
    "N bank lines to review"Imported lines not yet matched, categorized or excluded.
    "N records could not post to the books"A posting was refused, often because it falls in a closed period. Follow See why to Accounting.
    Amounts in bracketsNegative amounts.

    Common mistakes

    • Categorizing a customer payment instead of matching it. If the payment is already recorded in Receivables, match it. Categorizing it counts the income twice.
    • Closing a period before reconciling. Reconcile first, then lock.
    • Expecting to edit an entry. Entries do not change. Post a correcting entry instead.

    The books connect through the Accounting page; use that page to start or stop the built-in books and to see posting problems. Moving to an outside accounting system later means exporting the journal, disconnecting the books and connecting the new system. See Accounting connection and Features and editions.
