    Finance

    Business cases

    Model a new site, product, contract or capital project, compare scenarios, see when it pays back and send it for approval.

    On this page
    • The list
    • Create a business case
    • Edit the drivers
    • Read the results
    • Compare cases
    • Submit, approve, reopen
    • Plan against actual
    • Common mistakes

    A business case (also called a pro forma) is a spreadsheet-style model of an opportunity, kept inside the platform. You describe the revenue, costs and capital spending, and the system works out whether it is worth doing and when it pays for itself. You can try low, base and high scenarios, compare cases side by side, send the case for approval, and later check how the real results compare. Open Finance → Business cases.

    A business case is a model of your assumptions. It is not a forecast taken from your books.

    • See business cases and compare them: finance approvers, operations (plant) managers, property managers, executives and district managers (permission "view business cases").
    • Create, edit, submit, copy and reopen: finance approvers, operations managers and property managers (permission "manage business cases").
    • Approve: the approver named at the top of the page. By default this is the owner (the administrators); an administrator can name someone else.
    • Plan against actual: also needs permission to see the books.

    The list

    The page starts with a one-line summary of your cases. The Approved by line shows who approves cases. The table shows each case's number, title, kind, site, status, NPV, IRR, Payback, Funding needed and owner, all for the base case. Cases with a negative NPV have a red edge.

    NPV (net present value)
    What all the future cash is worth in today's money, after discounting at your discount rate. Positive means the case earns more than the rate; negative means it does not.
    IRR (internal rate of return)
    The yearly return the case earns on the cash put in. Shown as a dash if it cannot be worked out.
    Payback
    The number of months until cumulative cash turns positive. It reads No funding needed if cash never goes negative and Not within N years if it does not pay back in the period you modelled.
    Funding needed
    The lowest point cumulative cash reaches: the most you need to have available.

    Create a business case

    1. Click New business case.
    2. Enter a Title such as "Second store in Fort Worth".
    3. Choose the Kind: New site or store, New product or service, Customer contract, Capital project or Other opportunity.
    4. Choose a Site, or leave Company-wide (no site yet). Set the First month and the number of Years (1 to 10).
    5. Under Start from choose Example drivers to replace (a starter set for that kind of opportunity), This site's last 12 months (only if you can see that site's books) or Nothing.
    6. Click Create. The new case opens.

    Edit the drivers

    Click Edit. The figures at the top update as you type; they use the same calculation as the saved result, and your work is kept on the server as you go, so a closed tab loses nothing. Lines are in four sections:

    • Revenue
    • Cost of sales
    • Operating costs
    • Capital spending

    Each line has a label and a way of calculating: Units × price, Amount per month, % of revenue, People × annual cost or One-time amount (which methods are offered depends on the section). You can also set growth per year, the month it starts and ends, a ramp-up in months for revenue, and the life in years for capital.

    The settings above the lines are the Discount rate %, Tax rate % and Working capital (days of revenue).

    Scenarios

    The Scenarios table sets how the Low, Base and High cases differ: revenue %, costs %, capital % and a launch delay in months. Switch between them with the Low case, Base case and High case tabs. Everything on the page changes to show that scenario.

    Pull in numbers from elsewhere

    Above the lines, Pull in from adds lines drawn from the rest of the platform, which you then adjust: the site's books for the last 12 months, a budget, a CRM opportunity, a property's rent roll, an asset to replace, or the labor rate from work orders. Only sources you can see are offered.

    Click Save when finished, or Cancel to discard.

    Read the results

    The headline sentence gives the NPV and payback. The tiles show NPV, IRR, payback, funding needed, the First profitable month (the first month of positive operating profit before depreciation) and gross margin. Charts show cumulative cash and profit by year. A table gives profit and cash by year, and What moves the result pushes each driver down and up on its own and lists them by effect on NPV, largest first. This tells you which assumption to check hardest.

    The model uses straight-line depreciation, taxes profit after carrying losses forward, treats working capital as days of revenue (not released at the end) and discounts cash monthly.

    Compare cases

    1. On the list, tick Compare beside two or three cases.
    2. Click Compare N plans.
    3. Read the table of figures, the chart of cumulative cash and the table What differs, which lists only the assumptions that are not the same.

    You can also compare a case with one of its earlier revisions from Earlier revisions.

    Submit, approve, reopen

    1. When the case is ready, open it and click Submit for approval.
    2. The approver reviews it and approves or sends it back with a comment. Approval is a single step and a comment is required. The decision shows under Approvals.
    3. Approval freezes the lines and stores the approved base-case figures.

    To change a submitted or approved case, type a reason under Reopen and click Reopen. A submitted case is withdrawn from approval. An approved case starts a new revision that needs approval again, and the approved figures are kept so you can compare. Copy makes a new case from an existing one.

    StatusMeaning
    DraftBeing built or sent back. Editable.
    SubmittedWaiting for the approver. Reopen to withdraw it.
    ApprovedFrozen. Its base case is the plan.

    Plan against actual

    Once a case for a site is approved and its start month has come, a Plan against actual block compares the plan with the site's posted books, month by month: revenue, operating profit and capital spent, with the months completed. It needs access to the books to appear. Its headline says whether the site is on track, behind or well behind.

    Common mistakes

    • Leaving the example numbers in. The starter drivers are examples to replace.
    • Judging by NPV alone. Check Funding needed and the low case too.
    • Waiting for approval to model changes. Copy the case and test the change there.

    An administrator can name the approver, replacing the default of the owner, with Change beside Approved by. Visibility of the Business cases feature follows the feature switches. See Features and editions and Roles and access.
