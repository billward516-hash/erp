    Quality and safety

    Changes and notices

    Review and authorize changes to equipment, processes and procedures before use, and tell the right people with notices that track who has acknowledged.

    On this page
    • Request a change
    • Screening and review
    • If a change is rejected
    • What the stages mean
    • Do the action plan
    • Startup and closing
    • Change notices
    • Common mistakes

    Management of change (MOC) makes sure a change to equipment, operating limits, materials, software, procedures or staffing is reviewed by the right people before anyone uses it, and that the follow-up work (documents, training, work orders, tests) is done and checked. A change notice (an engineering change notice, or ECN, and similar) is how you tell colleagues, customers and suppliers about a change and keep a record of who has acknowledged. Both are on the Changes page, which also holds temporary SOP changes.

    • See changes and notices: most staff (permission "view changes"), including trainers and managers.
    • Request a change, answer the screening questions on your own request, start a temporary SOP change: operators, technicians, buyers and most managers (permission "request changes").
    • Act as change coordinator: maintenance managers and plant managers (permission "manage changes"). They link work orders, complete actions on other people's behalf, open the pre-startup review, close changes, raise and issue notices, and approve temporary SOP changes.
    • Review and authorize: the people holding the reviewing roles the change needs (safety and environmental, quality, maintenance and engineering), and finally the plant (operations) manager.

    Request a change

    Open Quality & safety → Changes and click Request a change.

    1. Choose the Facility and the Type of change: equipment, process, procedure, material, controls software, organizational, facility or other.
    2. Write a Title, What will change? and Why? (at least ten characters each for the last two).
    3. Under Planning the change, say whether it is Planned (nothing changes until approved), Emergency (already made to protect people, product or equipment) or Unintended (found after it happened).
    4. Describe the consequences, what it changes in the quality system (processes, documents, records), the resources it needs, and who is responsible. These four are required before review.
    5. Say whether customers are unaffected, must be told, or must approve it first. If they are affected, name them and what they must be told or approve.
    6. Optionally set a target date, tick Temporary change with a removal date (up to 180 days), and tick the affected equipment.
    7. Click Save draft.

    On the change page you can attach the presentation behind the justification (a PowerPoint or PDF, or an https link) while it is a draft. Every version of an uploaded deck is kept.

    Screening and review

    1. On the draft, answer all nine Screening questions Yes or No. They ask whether the change affects safety systems, introduces a new chemical or material, changes operating limits, affects controls software, product quality, the environment, procedures, training or staffing.
    2. Click Submit for review.

    The answers decide, by fixed rules (shown on the page as "Screened with"), the risk level (low, medium, high), which reviews are needed (safety and environmental, quality, maintenance and engineering), and whether a pre-startup safety review is required (it is for high risk, and for changes to safety systems or new chemicals). The system also generates the action plan: revise the affected documents, train people, link the work order that implements the change, brief the affected shifts, tell or get approval from customers, a hazard review for high risk, a function test for safety devices, a consequence review for emergency or unintended changes, and a restore action for temporary changes.

    Reviewers sign in order: first the review roles, then the plant manager authorizes. Each must record what was checked and any conditions. Nobody approves their own request. If a change is revised after approval, its approvals are voided and it must be approved again.

    If a change is rejected

    A reviewer who rejects must give a reason, and the change returns to its author for rework. The change page shows a red notice with who rejected it, in which review, when, and the reason.

    1. Open the rejected change. The requester, the owner or a change coordinator sees Rework the change.
    2. Edit the description, reason, planning answers, customers and target date to answer the reviewer, and click Save changes. You can also attach a better presentation (the justification deck can be replaced while the change is rejected). Saving returns the change to Draft as the next revision.
    3. Answer the screening questions again and click Resubmit for review. If nothing needs to change in the fields (for example you only attached the missing deck), you can resubmit straight away from the rejected state.

    Resubmitting starts a fresh approval round at the next revision number: all reviewers sign again, including those who had already approved, and the action plan generated from the screening is rebuilt (open generated actions are replaced, ones you added yourself stay). The earlier round is not erased: under Sign-offs every round is listed (Change authorization, round 1, round 2…) with its revision, each reviewer's decision and comment, and the History shows Reworked and Resubmitted.

    Do the action plan

    1. Open the approved change. Under Action plan each action shows what must be done before startup or before closing, its owner and due date.
    2. When the action is done, the owner or a change coordinator clicks Mark done.
    3. Some actions are verified against real records. A document action needs a revision approved after the change was authorized. A work order action needs the linked work order verified or closed (coordinators link it with Link a work order…). A training action needs an updated course version published and assigned. Others need a note of what was done.

    The action to restore a temporary change cannot be cancelled.

    Startup and closing

    1. A coordinator clicks Put into service once all startup actions are done. If a pre-startup review is required, the button reads Open pre-startup review instead.
    2. In the review, answer each of the seven questions Yes, No or N/A, explaining any answer other than Yes. Click Save answers. A No blocks startup until it is fixed. When every item is Yes or N/A, the startup sign-off goes to safety and operations.
    3. When the change is in service and all actions are done, a coordinator opens Close the change: summarise the outcome, say whether it achieved its purpose, and give the evidence (measurements, results, records).
    4. If it did not achieve its purpose, name the follow-up: another change or a corrective action, by its number, or use the link to open a corrective action from this change.

    The Register report button at the top of the Changes page lists all changes, including unplanned ones, for management review.

    Change notices

    Click the Change notices tab. Each notice lists what changed and who must be told, then tracks who has been sent it and who has acknowledged. Kinds: Engineering change notice, Product change notice, Supplier change, Deviation, Waiver, Bulletin and Other notice. The tiles show notices issued and open, those waiting for acknowledgements, and those past the acknowledgement date.

    To raise one from a change, open the change and click Raise a change notice. To raise one on its own, click New notice on the tab.

    1. Choose the kind and site. Set Effective on and Acknowledge by. Acknowledgements must be due on or before the effective date.
    2. Write the title, summary, and (optionally) what is changing: from, to, and drawings affected.
    3. Choose recipients: colleagues, customers, suppliers, and others (one per line). Add at least one before issuing.
    4. Click Save draft, check it, then click Issue the notice.

    Issuing sends an in-app notification to the colleagues listed. Outside parties are not emailed by the system: click PDF to send, send it yourself, then click Mark sent beside each one. An issued notice cannot be edited; cancel it (with a reason) and issue a corrected one.

    Acknowledgement

    ShownMeaning
    WaitingSent, not yet acknowledged, and not yet late.
    LateThe acknowledge-by date has passed.
    A date, by and howAcknowledged. For colleagues, they clicked it themselves. For others, a coordinator recorded who confirmed and how.
    1. If you are a recipient, open the notice and click I have read this.
    2. For a customer or supplier, a coordinator types Who confirmed, chooses email, phone, signed or other, and clicks Record.
    3. When everyone has acknowledged, the notice owner is told. The owner clicks Close notice. If anyone is still outstanding, a closing note saying why is required.

    Tick Only notices I still need to acknowledge on the tab to see your own. Recipients are reminded two days before the due date, on the day, and when late; the owner is told who they are still waiting for.

    Common mistakes

    • Making the change first. If it was an emergency, record it as one; the system adds a review of its consequences.
    • Vague planning fields. Reviewers rely on the consequences and resources; the submit is refused without them.
    • Closing a notice with people outstanding and no reason. The system asks for one.
    • Assuming a notice emails customers. It doesn't; send the PDF yourself.

    Reviewing roles (safety/environmental, quality, maintenance and engineering, and plant manager) must be assigned to real people at each site, or reviews cannot complete. Small sites can give one person several roles, but a request still needs two different signers. Notice and acknowledgement reminders come from the reminders catalogue. Management of change can be switched off per company. See Sites and people, Reminders and retention and Features and editions.
