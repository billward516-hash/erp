    Field service

    Field service

    Dispatch technicians to customers' equipment, manage agreements, trucks and tools, and bill the visits.

    On this page
    • Agreements
    • Call-outs
    • Visits and their statuses
    • The dispatch board
    • Technicians, trucks and tools
    • Billing
    • The service report and quoting repairs
    • Settings
    • Common mistakes

    Field service is for companies whose business is maintaining other people's equipment on a schedule. A customer signs a service agreement covering some equipment. The system generates visits on the agreed frequency. A coordinator (the dispatcher) assigns each visit to a technician. The technician does the work on a tablet (see Field service: My day), and the visit is billed.

    Field service is licensed per site. If your site does not have the field service licence, the menu is hidden and the pages refuse to open. Ask your administrator; see the box at the end.

    Who can do this: a service dispatcher (permission "manage field service") can create call-outs and agreements, assign and unassign visits, send the day to a technician, cancel visits, and manage technicians, trucks and settings. Field technicians have "execute field service" and use My day. Managing billing and creating invoices needs receivables authority ("view receivables" to see the Billing tab and "manage receivables" to create invoices). Making quotes needs the right to quote (service dispatchers and sales representatives).

    Open Field service → Field service. The tabs are Dispatch, Visits, Agreements, Technicians, trucks and tools, Billing and Settings. Billing shows only to people with receivables access, and Settings only to dispatch managers. A Performance report button at the top opens the service report; see Reports.

    Agreements

    An agreement is a contract with one customer for one servicing site. It lists the units it covers and how often each is serviced.

    1. On the Agreements tab, click New agreement.
    2. Choose the Customer and Servicing site. Set Starts and (optionally) Ends.
    3. Choose Billing: Per visit (enter a fee per visit) or Flat monthly (enter a monthly fee).
    4. Optionally set the Response time for call-outs (hours), tick Parts are included in the fee and Renews automatically, and add Terms.
    5. Click Save draft.
    6. On the agreement, under Add a covered unit, choose the equipment, name the service (for example "Quarterly PM"), set Every N weeks, months or years, the First due date, estimated minutes, any skill needed, a checklist (one item per line) and, if different, a fee for this unit. Click Add.
    7. Click Activate (generates the visits). At least one unit must be covered.

    Equipment must already belong to the customer. Visits are created ahead of time (30 days by default, see Settings), one per plan and date. If a plan fell behind, you get one overdue visit, not a pile.

    Agreement statusMeaning
    DraftBeing set up. No visits yet.
    ActiveVisits are being generated.
    SuspendedPaused with a reason. Activate resumes it.
    EndedFinished normally, with a reason.
    CancelledCancelled, with a reason.

    Suspend, End and Cancel each need a reason of at least three characters. To stop covering one unit use Remove beside it, and Restore to bring it back.

    Call-outs

    A call-out is an unscheduled visit for a breakdown.

    1. On the Visits tab, click New call-out.
    2. Choose the Customer, then the Equipment (add new equipment to the customer first).
    3. Set Priority, Needed by, Minutes and any Skill needed.
    4. Describe What is wrong and add notes.
    5. Click Create call-out.

    The visit appears under To schedule on the dispatch board with a Call-out tag.

    Visits and their statuses

    The Visits tab lists visits (filter Open visits, Overdue or All). Click one to open it.

    StatusMeaning
    To scheduleExists, not yet assigned.
    ScheduledAssigned to a technician on a day. The technician has been told.
    DispatchedThe day has been sent to the technician.
    On siteThe technician started the visit.
    DoneThe service report was submitted.
    CancelledCancelled with a reason.

    An Overdue tag appears when the due date has passed. To cancel a visit that is not yet started, type a reason (at least three characters) and click Cancel visit.

    The dispatch board

    The Dispatch tab shows one day. Use the arrows, the date or Today to change day. There is one card per technician, showing:

    • How full the day is as a percentage (green, amber over 85%, red if overbooked), or why they are not working (for example time off).
    • Their skills, and minutes on site plus minutes driving against the day's capacity.
    • Their stops: arrival time, visit, customer and site, and drive minutes.

    Under the cards, To schedule lists visits with no technician in the next week, with customer, what, due date, minutes and skill.

    Assign a visit

    1. In To schedule, click Assign… on a visit.
    2. A panel ranks the technicians. For each you see Can take it or the reasons they cannot (missing skill, not working that day, time off, too many booked hours), Extra drive, visits already that day and minutes free afterward.
    3. Click Assign beside a technician who can take it.

    Assigning creates the visit's work order, assigns the technician to it and notifies them. The system picks a start time: after the technician's previous stop plus drive time, or the start of their day. It refuses a visit that would run past the end of the day. To undo, click Unassign on the stop; it goes back to To schedule.

    Send the day

    When a technician's day is right, click Send the day to NAME. All their scheduled visits for that day change to Dispatched.

    How drive time is estimated

    The system does not use a maps service. Drive time is the straight-line distance between stops multiplied by a road factor, divided by an average speed. Both are typed in Settings (the note above the board shows the current values). A stop with a star after the minutes is an estimate because a site or technician has no map coordinates; the system then assumes 20 minutes. Treat all drive times as a guide for ranking people, not a promise to the customer.

    Technicians, trucks and tools

    • Technicians: shows name, skills, working hours, truck and visits today. A dispatcher turns an employee into a technician by choosing them, entering Skills (comma separated) and optional home latitude and longitude, and clicking Save technician.
    • Trucks: add a vehicle with a tag, name and plate (Add truck). Each truck has its own stock location, and parts used on visits come off it, never below zero.
    • Restock list: parts on a truck at or below their minimum, how many are needed to reach the par level, and how many the storerooms can supply. A restock is an ordinary stock transfer.
    • Tools: assets of class tool, with calibration due date. Check out to me and Return track who holds each. A tool overdue for calibration, out of service, or that failed its last calibration cannot be checked out.

    Billing

    When a technician submits a service report, the report is marked for billing. The rules:

    Kind of workWhat is billed
    Per-visit agreementThe unit's fee (or the agreement's per-visit fee), plus parts at cost plus the company markup, unless parts are included.
    Flat monthly agreementThe visit itself is covered. Only parts that are not included are billed.
    Call-outTime and materials: hours at the company labor rate, plus parts.

    Billing status on the report is Ready to invoice, Covered by the agreement or billed. The technician and dispatcher do not create the invoice; someone with receivables authority does.

    1. Open the Billing tab. Visits ready to invoice lists each visit and amount.
    2. Click Create invoice on a row (or on the visit's own page). A visit can be invoiced once.
    3. For flat monthly fees, pick the Month and click Create the month's invoices. This creates one invoice per flat-fee agreement, the full fee from the month the agreement starts, with no proration. Running a month twice is safe.

    Invoices continue in Receivables.

    The service report and quoting repairs

    On a done visit, open it to see the service report: work performed, findings, hours, checklist issues, whether the customer signed (with a View signature link) or why not, readings and billing status. If the technician listed recommended repairs, they appear in a table.

    1. Tick the repairs to quote. Enter a Price each where the technician left it blank.
    2. Click Create a draft quote from the selected repairs. The draft quote opens in Quotes and orders.

    A repair already on a quote shows "On QUOTE-NUMBER" and cannot be quoted twice. Dispatchers are notified when a report has repairs to quote.

    Settings

    The Settings tab (dispatch managers) shows and edits: road factor (1 to 3), average speed (5 to 80 mph), generate visits days ahead (1 to 365), default visit minutes, call-out labor rate per hour and parts markup percent (0 to 500). Click Change, edit, Save.

    Common mistakes

    • Activating an agreement with no covered units. At least one is needed.
    • Expecting a visit to appear for equipment that is not the customer's. Add the equipment to the customer first.
    • Forgetting to Send the day. Visits stay Scheduled until sent.
    • Waiting for the invoice to appear. Someone with receivables rights must create it.
    • The visit's work order is completed by the technician and still has to be verified by a second person like any other. See Work orders.

    Administrators: field service is switched on per site through a licence entitlement. Without it the module returns an error ("not licensed for this site") and the menu is hidden. Check the entitlement, then give dispatchers the Service Dispatcher role and technicians the Field Technician role. See Features and editions, Sites and people and Roles and access. Customers and their sites and equipment are described in Customers.
