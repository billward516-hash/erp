    Reference

    Glossary

    Plain-language meanings of the words, short forms and technical terms used across the platform and this guide.

    On this page
    • Maintenance, stores and production
    • Quality, safety and change
    • Buying, selling and money
    • The platform and its administration

    This page explains the words and short forms you will meet in the menus and in this guide. Where a term is a general industry word, the meaning given is the everyday one; where the platform uses it in a particular way, that is said. Terms are in alphabetical order within each section. Use your browser's search (Ctrl+F) to find one.

    Maintenance, stores and production

    Asset
    A machine, vehicle, building system or other thing the company owns and keeps working, such as a press, a boiler or a walk-in cooler. Each has a tag (its number), a location and a history. See Assets.
    Availability
    The share of scheduled time a machine was able to run. Worked out from the states people record (running, down and why).
    Bill of material (BOM)
    The list of parts and quantities needed to make one finished product. Lines imported into the platform land on a draft revision that a person releases.
    Bin
    A shelf or location inside a storeroom where a part is kept.
    Condition reading
    A measured value logged against a piece of equipment, such as a cooler temperature, compared with its limits. An out-of-limit reading opens an excursion and a request.
    Corrective action
    Work done to fix the cause of a problem so that it does not come back. See CAPA.
    Downtime
    Time when a machine was not running, with a reason such as mechanical failure or waiting for material. Planned downtime (PM, setup) is separated from unplanned.
    Kit
    The set of parts a planned job needs, gathered ahead of time. A kit is “short” if some parts are not in stock.
    Lot
    A batch of a part received or made together, tracked as a group, usually with an expiry date. A serial number tracks one individual unit.
    Meter reading
    A count of hours, cycles or distance on a machine. Meter-based PM plans are triggered by it.
    Part
    A spare, material or finished item with a part number, a unit of measure and (optionally) a cost.
    PM (preventive maintenance)
    Scheduled upkeep done before something breaks, by calendar (every 30 days) or by meter (every 500 hours). A PM plan creates work orders when due. See Preventive maintenance.
    Production order
    An instruction to make a quantity of a product by following its routing. See Production orders.
    Repair or replace
    A report that compares what a machine has cost to keep running with the value of replacing it.
    Routing
    The ordered operations (steps) used to make a part, with the work center for each and its setup and run times.
    Scrap, rework
    Pieces made wrongly. Scrap is thrown away; rework is fixed and used.
    Shift log
    The note handed from one shift to the next, with facts compiled by the system and items carried forward.
    Space
    A unit, suite, room or common area inside a property, tracked as an asset of that kind.
    Storeroom
    A place where parts are kept and counted; every receipt, issue and count goes through the stock ledger.
    Stock ledger
    The permanent record of every movement of stock. Balances are the sum of the movements, and the ledger is never edited; corrections are new movements.
    Work center
    A machine group or manual station where an operation is done, with its working hours.
    Work order (WO)
    A job to repair, inspect or maintain something: who does it, what for, what parts and time were used and when it was verified. See Work orders.

    Quality, safety and change

    8D
    “Eight disciplines”: a structured way of solving a problem in eight steps, from forming a team to preventing it from happening again. Run inside a CAPA. See CAPA and 8D.
    Audit (quality)
    A planned check of a process or supplier against a standard, with findings that become actions.
    Calibration
    Checking and adjusting a measuring instrument against a known standard. An instrument past its calibration date cannot be used for production measurements.
    CAPA
    Corrective and preventive action. A record that takes a problem from its root cause through fixes to a check that they worked. See CAPA and 8D.
    Containment
    Quick action to stop a problem spreading while the cause is found, such as holding suspect stock.
    Disposition
    The decision on nonconforming material: use as is, rework, scrap, return to supplier.
    ECN (engineering change notice)
    A notice that a design, specification or process has changed. In the platform, notices (ECN, PCN, supplier change, deviation, waiver, bulletin) are tracked from sent to acknowledged for each recipient. See Changes and notices.
    FMEA
    Failure mode and effects analysis: a worksheet that lists how something could fail, how bad it would be, how likely and how likely it is to be caught, to rank the risks.
    Incident, near miss
    An incident is a safety event where someone was hurt or something was damaged; a near miss could have been but was not. See Safety.
    ISO 9001
    An international standard for quality management systems. Many customers ask to see evidence of it.
    MOC (management of change)
    A review-and-approve process for changes to equipment, processes or procedures before they go live, including a pre-startup check. See Changes and notices.
    NCR (nonconformance report)
    A record that something did not meet its requirements: a bad part, a missed step. It is contained, given a disposition and may lead to a CAPA. See Quality.
    PSSR (pre-startup safety review)
    A final check, part of a change, that everything is ready and safe before restarting.
    Qualification
    Proof that a person is authorised to do a type of work. Completing a course does not grant one; an authorised person does.
    Read-across
    Checking whether a problem found in one place could also exist elsewhere (another machine, line or site).
    SCAR
    Supplier corrective action request: asks a supplier to find and fix the cause of a problem with what they delivered.
    SPC (statistical process control)
    Charting measurements over time against calculated control limits to see when a process drifts before parts go out of tolerance. See SPC control charts.
    Temporary change
    A procedure change that is allowed for a stated period and then ends. See Temporary SOP changes.

    Buying, selling and money

    Accounts payable (AP), payables
    What the company owes its suppliers. See Payables.
    Accounts receivable (AR), receivables
    What customers owe the company. See Receivables.
    Aging
    A list of unpaid invoices grouped by how overdue they are (current, 30, 60, 90 days).
    Bill
    A supplier's invoice to the company (also called a supplier invoice).
    Books
    The built-in ledger of accounts and journal entries, available in every edition. See Books.
    Business case (pro forma)
    A projection of profit, cash, payback, net present value and rate of return for an investment, with scenarios and finance approval. See Business cases.
    Chart of accounts
    The numbered list of accounts the books use (cash, receivables, travel expense and so on).
    Credit memo
    A document that reduces what a customer owes, for a return or correction.
    Journal entry
    A balanced posting to the books (debits equal credits). Entries never change; a correction is a reversal plus a new entry.
    Purchase order (PO)
    The company's order to a supplier. A purchase request asks for one and goes through approval first. See Purchasing.
    Reconciliation
    Checking that the bank's statement agrees with the books for a period.
    RFQ (request for quote)
    Asking suppliers for prices on the same thing (Supplier RFQs), or a customer's request to the company for a price (Customer RFQs).
    Three-way match
    Checking that the purchase order, the goods received and the supplier's invoice agree before the invoice is paid.
    Trial balance
    A list of every account's balance, used to prove debits equal credits. Also what a previous system exports as opening balances.

    The platform and its administration

    Administrator
    The role that holds every permission, including the administration screens. See Your first week.
    API token
    A long code beginning erp_ that stands in for a password for scripts and trials. See Sign-in and access tokens.
    Approval chain
    The steps a record such as an expense report or purchase request passes through, set by rules on amount, department and site.
    Archive (company)
    A full copy of the company's data, taken every 30 days by default, used to bring records back or roll the company back. See Archives and recovery.
    Audit trail
    The permanent record of who changed what and when. It is a hash chain: each event carries a fingerprint of the one before, so tampering shows.
    Daily job
    The housekeeping the server does once a day: reminders, digests, retention, archives. See Scheduled jobs.
    Edition (Lite, Standard)
    The size of plan the company has. Lite is bookkeeping and company basics for very small companies; Standard has the full set. See Features and editions.
    Employee, user
    An employee is a person in the directory; a user is someone who can sign in. They are linked by employee number but are separate things.
    Entitlement
    A licensed module switched on for a site. Field service is an entitlement per site.
    Facility
    An older word for site, still used in some screens and error messages.
    Feature
    An area of the platform that an administrator can show or hide by role. See Features and editions.
    Fit interview
    A short questionnaire that recommends features and plan depth. See Implementation planner.
    Group (site group)
    A named set of sites (portfolio, region, district) to which a role can be granted in one go.
    Idempotency key
    A label a phone or script sends with a change so that if it has to retry, the change is applied once. Saved replies are kept 7 days.
    Import profile (saved mapping)
    A saved record of how a source file's columns map to platform fields, reused for the next file from the same system.
    Legacy ID
    The identifier a record had in the previous system. Re-imports use it, with the source system's name, to update records instead of duplicating them.
    OIDC (OpenID Connect)
    The standard behind “sign in with your company account”. See Sign-in and access tokens.
    Permission, role
    A permission is one thing a person may do. A role is a bundle of permissions. See Permissions table.
    Practice copy
    A separate company used to rehearse imports without touching real data.
    Retention
    How long short-lived working data is kept before the daily clean-up removes it. See Reminders and retention.
    RLS (row-level security)
    A database feature that decides, row by row, who may see a record. Here it guarantees one company can never read another's data, even through a program error.
    Rollback (company)
    Replacing the company's business data with that of an archive. Needs a second administrator.
    Single sign-on (SSO)
    Signing in through the company's identity provider instead of a separate password.
    Site
    A plant, store, warehouse, office or property. Most records belong to one. See Sites and people.
    Sync (accounting)
    Sending queued bills, invoices and expense reports to the books or an export file. See Accounting connection.
    Tenant, company
    One customer of the platform and all its data. The guide says “company”; error codes and technical notes may say “tenant”.
