    Improvements

    Suggest an idea, follow it from approval to a measured before-and-after, and have the savings checked by someone else before it is closed.

    On this page
    • Suggest an improvement
    • The stages
    • Carry an improvement through (owners and leads)
    • Common mistakes

    The Improvements page (Company → Improvements) is for ideas that make work safer, cheaper, faster or simpler. Anyone can suggest one. A lead approves it and names an owner; the owner measures how things were before, makes the change, measures after, and someone else checks the savings before it is closed. The aim is to count real results, not hopes.

    Everyone with a login can suggest an improvement. Leads (maintenance managers, plant managers, production supervisors, quality managers, executives and administrators in the starter roles) decide on ideas and validate savings. The owner a lead names carries the idea out.

    Suggest an improvement

    1. Open Company → Improvements and choose Suggest an improvement.
    2. Choose the Site it relates to and a Category (Safety, Quality, Cost, Delivery, Maintenance, Energy, Workplace or Other).
    3. Give it a short Title, such as “Shadow board for press tools”.
    4. In Problem, say what goes wrong today and how often. Facts and numbers help the lead decide.
    5. In Idea, say how you would fix it. This is optional.
    6. Choose Send. The idea gets a number and leads at that site are notified. When it is decided, you are told whether it was approved or not taken forward, with the lead's note.

    The list shows four counts at the top: ideas waiting, in progress, closed, and the validated savings per year so far. Click any row to open the improvement.

    The stages

    The bar at the top of each improvement shows where it is.

    StageMeaningWho moves it on
    IdeaSuggested; waiting for a decision.A lead chooses Approve (and names an Owner) or Decline (a note is required).
    ApprovedAccepted, with an owner. The plan and the baseline are still to do.The owner or a lead.
    Baseline setThe “before” measurement exists.The owner or a lead chooses The change is in place (today).
    Implemented, measuringThe change is made; the “after” is being measured.The owner measures the result; a different lead validates the savings.
    ClosedSavings validated. The idea is finished.—
    Not taken forwardDeclined, with a reason.—

    Carry an improvement through (owners and leads)

    1. Decide

    1. Open an improvement at stage Idea. Under Decision choose the owner and optionally add a note.
    2. Choose Approve, or write why not in the note and choose Decline. The owner is notified.

    2. Plan how to measure it

    1. Under Measure, choose What is measured:
      • Unplanned downtime of an asset (from records): pick the asset. The system measures from its recorded downtime.
      • Scrap rate at a work center (from records): pick the work center. The system measures from production records.
      • Something else, entered by hand: name the metric and its unit, and say whether lower is better. You type the baseline and the result.
    2. Optionally set a Target, an Est. savings / yr and a One-time cost. Choose Save the plan.

    Once a baseline is recorded, the kind of measurement can no longer be changed, so the before and after stay comparable.

    3. Measure the baseline

    Choose Measure the baseline (last 8 weeks) for a measurement from records, or enter a value and choose Record the baseline for a hand-entered one. The first shows the date window it used. You can measure it again before the change is made.

    4. Make the change

    Do the work. Add the next steps under Actions (an owner and a due date each) so they show up in the owner's My actions on Home. When the change is in place, choose The change is in place (today). It cannot be dated in the future, nor before the baseline window.

    5. Measure the result

    1. For a measurement from records, the system makes you wait at least four weeks after the change so a good week is not mistaken for a result. Until then the page says when the result can be measured.
    2. Choose Measure the result (or enter a value for a hand-entered metric). The page shows before, target, after, the percentage change, and whether the target was met.

    6. Validate the savings

    A lead who is not the owner enters the Validated savings / yr and a note on how it was worked out, then chooses Validate and close. Savings of 0 are allowed. The improvement shows who validated it, when and how. Only validated savings count toward the total at the top of the list; estimates are shown in grey as “est.”.

    Common mistakes

    • A vague problem. “Press is slow” gives a lead nothing to decide on. Say what, where and how often.
    • Skipping the baseline. Without a before, there is nothing to compare. The system will not let you mark the change in place until the baseline is recorded.
    • Owner validating their own savings. Not allowed; ask another lead.
    • Measuring too early. The four-week wait is deliberate.
    • Not seeing the menu entry. Improvements may be hidden for your role by your company. See why some menu items are missing.

    Improvements can be switched on or off per role under Admin → Features (see Administrator brief: features and editions). Measuring downtime and scrap from records needs those records to exist: assets with recorded states, and production entries with scrap. The Improvements report appears under Reports.
