    Permissions table

    Every permission the system checks, in plain words, and which starter roles hold it.

    On this page
    • How permissions work
    • The starter roles
    • The permissions
    • See also

    A permission is one thing a person may do, such as approving a timesheet or releasing a production order. A role is a named bundle of permissions. This page lists all 92 permissions and, for each, the starter roles that hold it. It is generated from the product's permission list, so it matches what the system checks.

    How permissions work

    • Every new company starts with 25 roles (listed below). The Administrator role holds every permission, including tenant.admin.
    • A person is given a role at one site, on a group of sites, or at all sites (leave the site blank when granting). A permission only works at the sites where a role covering it was granted. See Sites and people and Roles and access.
    • A person with several roles gets the combined permissions of all of them, site by site.
    • Hiding a feature under Features removes that feature's permissions from the roles it is hidden from (the setting is cached for up to about 30 seconds). The permission is still in the role, but the system treats it as not held.
    • Some actions are decided by a step in an approval chain rather than by a permission. Expenses and purchase requests are examples: the approver named in the rule can sign even without the matching manage permission. Nobody can approve their own timesheet or expense report (except in a single-person company where the licence allows it).
    • The starter roles cannot be edited. An administrator can make company roles under Admin → People and access → Roles by ticking permissions from this list (only ones the administrator holds; tenant.admin is the exception). Company roles are not listed below, because every company has its own; the Roles tab shows them with their permissions. Lite has the starter roles only.

    To see what a particular person can do, add up the roles they were granted, then look each permission up here. The Administrator column is left out of the tables because the Administrator role holds everything.
