    Production

    Planning

    See whether your work centers and materials can meet customer forecasts and firm orders, and act on the shortfalls.

    On this page
    • Reading the Plan tab
    • Turning the plan into action
    • Try a scenario
    • Assumptions the plan made
    • Forecasts
    • Calendars (managers)
    • Consumables (managers)
    • Common mistakes

    The Planning page turns customer forecasts and firm sales orders into two answers. First, how loaded each work center will be, month by month, against the hours it can really deliver. Second, which purchased materials you will be short of, and the date by which you must order them. You can try "what if" scenarios without changing anything, and you can turn the plan into purchase requests and production orders with one click.

    • See the plan and forecasts: plant managers, production supervisors/planners, buyers, sales staff and executives (permission "view planning").
    • Create scenarios, draft purchase requests, create production orders, activate forecasts, edit calendars and consumption rates: plant managers and production supervisors/planners (permission "manage planning"). Creating orders also needs the production manage permission; drafting a purchase request needs the purchasing request permission.

    Open Production → Planning. Pick the site in the box at the top right (only plant sites are listed). Tabs: Plan, Forecasts, and, for managers, Calendars and Consumables.

    Reading the Plan tab

    Choose a Horizon of 3, 6, 9 or 12 months. The tiles at the top summarise the plan:

    Peak load
    The busiest work center in the busiest month, as a percentage of its capacity.
    Work-center months over
    How many work center and month combinations need more than 100 percent of capacity.
    Materials short
    How many purchased parts you will not have enough of in the horizon.
    Operator shortfall
    Shown only when work centers require qualifications. Counts work-center months where there are not enough qualified people.
    Firm share of demand
    How much of the demand is firm sales orders rather than forecast.

    Work-center load

    Each cell shows a percentage and "hours needed / hours available". The colour shows how tight it is.

    ColourLoadMeaning
    Green85 percent or lessComfortable.
    AmberOver 85 up to 100 percentTight. Little room for breakdowns or rush orders.
    RedOver 100 percentOverloaded. The plan cannot be met without more capacity or less demand.

    Hover over a cell to see the pieces: hours from open production orders, firm orders, forecast, and the capacity after preventive maintenance. A line under the work center name shows its hours per week, number of machines or crews, and availability.

    • Load is open production orders plus planned production (firm and forecast), run through each part's released routing.
    • Capacity is the calendar hours times the machines or crews, times the availability the machines have actually demonstrated (from their recorded state), less preventive maintenance hours projected from the machines' calendar PM plans.
    • In each month, for a customer and part, the larger of the forecast and the firm orders counts, so firm orders above the forecast are never lost.

    Qualified operators

    If work centers require qualifications, a second table shows, per month, either "covered" or "N h short" of qualified people. A work center that depends on one person (or nobody) gets a tag. A list below names people whose qualification expires within the horizon and how many qualified people the work center will then have. People are shared: someone qualified on two work centers is counted once. See Training and performance.

    Materials

    The table lists each purchased part the plan needs, with the quantity available now, needed, on order, short, already requested, the date it is needed by, and the Order by date. A red past tag means the order-by date has already gone by. The For column says whether the demand comes from production, consumption (coolant, inserts, chemicals) or preventive maintenance parts.

    Turning the plan into action

    These buttons appear only for the plan "as planned", never when a scenario is selected, and only if you have the permissions above.

    1. To buy what is short, click Draft a purchase request for N short materials above the Materials table. A draft request opens for you to review and submit for approval. The shortfalls already requested or on order are not repeated. See Purchasing.
    2. To make something, find the part and month in the Planned production table and click create order. A planned production order is created. Release it when you are ready. See Production orders.

    Try a scenario

    A scenario changes the plan on screen without changing any real data. Use it to ask "what if demand rises 15 percent" or "what if we add a second shift on the lathes".

    1. Click New scenario.
    2. Give it a name, for example "Second shift on lathes".
    3. Enter a Forecast change in percent (for example 15 or -10).
    4. Optionally pick a work center and enter extra hours per week and extra machines or crews.
    5. Click Save and show. The tiles now show the new peak load and note what it was before.

    Choose As planned (no scenario) in the scenario box to go back to the real plan.

    Assumptions the plan made

    If the plan had to guess, for example a work center without a calendar, it lists the guesses under Assumptions this plan had to make. Read this before relying on the numbers.

    Forecasts

    The Forecasts tab lists the customer forecasts for the site: customer, version, status, months covered, total quantity, date received and source. Click one to see the quantities per part and month. Each cell shows the change from the version before it, and the firm sales-order quantity in that month. A red "firm" figure means firm orders already exceed the forecast.

    StatusMeaning
    DraftImported but not yet used by the plan.
    ActiveThe version the plan uses for that customer.
    SupersededAn earlier version, replaced when a newer one was activated. Kept for history.
    1. Import the customer's forecast (see the administrator note).
    2. Open the new draft and compare it with the previous version.
    3. Managers click Use this version in the plan.

    Calendars (managers)

    For each work center, set the hours a week it is scheduled per machine or crew (two 8-hour shifts, five days, is 80), the number of machines or crews (default: the machines assigned), and the operators per machine hour (default 1). Enter the values and click Save on that row. A blank hours-per-week field is treated as 40 hours, and the plan will list that as an assumption.

    Consumables (managers)

    Coolant, cutting inserts, chemicals and gases are used in proportion to something. Add a rate so the plan buys them in time.

    1. Under Add a rate, search for the part and click it.
    2. Enter the quantity and the base, for example "1 per 120 units made", "0.5 per 10 machine hours" or "2 per 1 month".
    3. Choose a work center, or the whole site. Add a note naming the source of the rate.
    4. Click Add. A new rate for the same part, work center and basis replaces the old one. Use Remove to drop a rate.

    The box What the stockroom shows compares your typed rates with what the stockroom actually issued over the last 90 days. Where they differ, Use this rate saves the observed monthly figure as a site-wide rate.

    Common mistakes

    • Trusting a plan with no calendars. Without a calendar, the work center is assumed to run 40 hours a week.
    • Forgetting to activate a new forecast. The plan keeps using the old version until a manager activates the new one.
    • Looking for the create buttons in a scenario. Orders and requests are made from the plan as it stands. Clear the scenario first.

    Customer forecasts, consumables usage sheets and bills of materials are loaded with Data import. See Importing data. Planning depends on production routings and can be switched off per company in Features and editions.
