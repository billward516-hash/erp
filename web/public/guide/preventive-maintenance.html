    Maintenance

    Preventive maintenance

    Plans that create work orders on a calendar or a meter, so routine upkeep happens before things break.

    On this page
    • Where to find it
    • The PM schedule
    • Calendar plans and meter plans
    • Create a plan
    • Generate work orders
    • A plan's page
    • Common mistakes

    Preventive maintenance (PM) is routine work done on a schedule: lubricate every month, change filters every 500 run hours. A PM plan says what to do, on which asset, how often, and which parts it needs. When a plan comes due, the system makes a normal work order for it.

    Who can do this: anyone who can view work orders can see the schedule and the plans. Maintenance managers, facilities managers and district managers can create plans, pause or resume them, and generate work orders (permission "manage work orders"). Technicians and operators with meter access can record meter readings from the asset page.

    Where to find it

    Open Maintenance → PM. If you do not see it, your company has the preventive maintenance feature turned off, or you lack access to work orders.

    The PM schedule

    The page shows plans due now or in the next 60 days. Four counters across the top summarise the state of play:

    • Overdue: plans past their due date or reading.
    • Due now: plans inside their lead window.
    • Due without a work order: due or overdue plans nobody has started a job for.
    • Short of parts: plans whose parts list is not fully in stock at the site.

    The table lists plan code, site, asset, interval, next due, state, the current work order (if any) and a parts check (In stock or N short). Overdue rows are marked red.

    StateMeaning
    Not dueThe due date or reading is still beyond the lead window.
    DueInside the lead window. It is time to plan the job.
    OverdueThe due date has passed, or the meter has reached the due reading.
    PausedThe plan has been switched off. It does not generate work orders.

    Calendar plans and meter plans

    Calendar
    Due every N days. It is due from the due date minus the lead window in days.
    Meter reading
    Due every N units of a meter (run hours, cycles). It is due once the latest recorded reading reaches the due reading minus the lead amount. Readings are entered on the asset page; see Assets: record a meter reading.

    The plan also has a schedule basis:

    Count from when work is done
    The next due point is one interval after the work is finished. A late job pushes everything back.
    Keep fixed dates
    The next due point is one interval after the previous due point, skipping any cycle you have already passed. Dates keep their rhythm even when a job runs late.

    Create a plan

    1. On the PM page, click New plan.
    2. Choose the Asset.
    3. Give it a Plan code that is unique (for example PM-CNC-102-M) and a Title.
    4. Choose the Trigger: Calendar or Meter reading.
    5. For a calendar plan, enter Every (days). For a meter plan, enter the Meter name and Every (meter units).
    6. Optionally set the First due date (or first due reading). If you leave it blank, the first due point is one interval from now.
    7. Choose the Schedule basis (see above).
    8. Enter Estimated hours if you know them.
    9. Under Parts list, click Add a part for each part and quantity the job uses.
    10. Click Create plan. The plan opens.

    Use the same meter name every time (for example always run_hours). The asset page suggests the meter names already in use.

    Generate work orders

    Work orders from PM are made when you ask for them, not automatically in the background.

    1. On the PM page, click Generate due work orders.
    2. A green message lists what was created (work order number and plan), and which plans were skipped because they already have an open work order.

    Generation makes one work order per due plan and never a duplicate. Each is a preventive work order titled "PM: ..." with the plan's parts planned on it. If the parts are stocked at the site, the stock is reserved in a kit so it is waiting when work starts. Technicians then work the job as usual.

    The plan only moves forward when its work order is verified, not when it is completed. If nobody verifies, the plan stays due and, once the job is closed, will be due again. See Work orders: verify.

    A plan's page

    Click a plan to see its schedule: trigger and basis, next due, lead window, last done (date and reading), estimated time and procedure document. Beside it is the parts list with On hand at site and a Short flag. Below is the history of work orders from the plan, each marked On time or Late.

    Pause or resume a plan

    1. Open the plan.
    2. Type a reason in the box (at least five characters, for example "asset idle until Q1").
    3. Click Pause plan (or Resume plan when it is paused).

    The reason is kept in the audit trail.

    Common mistakes

    • Waiting for work orders to appear on their own. Press Generate due work orders regularly, or rely on the reminder (below).
    • Forgetting to enter meter readings. A meter plan with no reading yet never becomes due.
    • Completing the work order but never verifying it, so the plan does not advance.
    • Planning a part that is not stocked at the site. The plan shows not stocked, and the job's kit is not built.

    Administrators: a daily reminder, "PM plans due without a work order", goes to maintenance planners at the site when a plan is inside its lead window and has no open work order. You can change or turn it off under Reminders and retention. The whole PM area can be hidden under Features and editions.
