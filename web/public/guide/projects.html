    Service companies

    Projects

    Plan a project with a budget, a team and milestones, book hours and expenses to it, and see cost, margin and what is at risk while you can still change it.

    On this page
    • Create a project
    • Status
    • Team

    • Milestones
    • Contract value and change orders
    • Booking hours and expenses
    • Cost rates
    • Alerts and reports

    A project is work done for a customer (or inside the company) with a budget, a team, tasks and milestones. People book hours from their timesheet and expenses from their expense report to the project. The project page then shows how the hours compare with the budget, and, for people who may see cost, what the work has cost and what it earns. Open it from Service companies → Projects.

    Projects are part of the Standard edition; the Lite edition does not include them. An administrator can turn the feature off for some roles under Admin → Features.

    • See projects: people with the permission to view projects at the site (project managers, operations managers, finance approvers, executives, sales managers, billing clerks, administrators). The project’s owner and the people on its team always see that project, whatever their role.
    • Create and change projects, tasks, team, milestones and change orders: project managers, operations managers and administrators at the site, and the owner of the project itself.
    • See cost, margin and cost rates: only people with the separate cost permission (finance approvers, executives and administrators in the starter setup). Everyone else gets the same pages without those figures.

    Create a project

    1. Open Projects and choose New project.
    2. Give it a Name, the Customer and the Billing type. Only an internal, non-billable project can have no customer.
    3. For a fixed-price project enter the Contract amount.
    4. Set the Owner (you, if you leave it), the dates and the Hours budget. People with the cost permission also see Cost budget.
    5. Tick Any employee may book time to this project if everyone may book to it; otherwise only the team can.
    6. Choose Create project. It gets a number such as PRJ-000001, starts Active (or Proposed if you chose that), and the owner is put on the team if they are an employee.
    Billing typeMeaning
    Time and materialsThe customer pays for the hours and expenses.
    Fixed priceThe customer pays an agreed contract amount, usually by milestone. The contract can be changed only by approved change orders.
    RetainerA recurring arrangement. Projects of this type can be booked to like any other.
    Non-billable (internal)Hours are never billable. Use it for internal work, such as training or business development.

    Billing the customer (rates, billing runs, invoices) is a separate step. This page records what is billable and what the project has cost; it does not create invoices.

    StatusMeaningCan book hours and expenses?

    Use the buttons at the top of the project (Start, Put on hold, Resume, Mark completed, Cancel project). The reason you give is kept in the audit trail.

    Team

    On the Team tab, choose a person and an optional role such as “Senior consultant”, then Add to team. Remove takes someone off the team (they can no longer book to the project; their past hours stay). A project that is open to all employees lets everyone book, but the team still lists who is assigned.

    Tasks and budgets

    On the Budget and actual tab, add tasks or phases with Add task: a name, an hours budget, whether the task is billable, and (with the cost permission) a cost budget. A closed task no longer appears on timesheets.

    The project’s hours budget is the number on the project if you entered one, otherwise the sum of its task budgets.

    FigureHow it is worked out
    Actual hoursHours on approved timesheets. Hours still in draft, submitted or sent back are shown as Waiting, so the figures do not move while someone is still editing.
    UsedActual divided by budget. Over 100% is over budget.
    Billable shareBillable actual hours divided by all actual hours.
    PaceApproved hours in the last 28 days, as hours a week. If hours are left in the budget, the page shows the date they would run out at that pace.
    Cost (cost permission only)Each approved hour at the person’s cost rate on the day it was worked, plus approved and reimbursed expenses named for the project. Hours with no rate in force cost nothing and are listed as such, so you know to set a rate.
    BilledWhat has been invoiced to the project. Until billing is in use this is 0.
    Margin (cost permission only)Billed less cost. A project not yet invoiced shows a negative margin.

    Warnings appear at the top of the project: near the budget (at the company’s alert share, 90% to start), over the budget, a past end date while still active, late milestones, and hours with no cost rate.

    A cost budget is cost information. It is shown, and can be changed, only by people with the cost permission, and the audit trail records that it changed but not the figures.

    Milestones

    On the Milestones tab, add a milestone with a name and due date. On a fixed-price project give either a fixed Amount or a percent of contract; percents add up to at most 100%, and the amounts cannot exceed the contract value. A percent follows the contract value, so an approved change order moves it.

    StatusMeaning
    PlannedNot yet reached. Past its due date it is shown as late and appears in What is late.
    ReachedThe work is delivered. Billing can now invoice it.
    BilledInvoiced by a billing run. A billed milestone cannot be changed.

    Only the project’s owner or a project manager can choose Mark reached; it is recorded with who and when. If it was marked by mistake, Take back returns it to planned with a reason (not once it is billed).

    Contract value and change orders

    The Contract tab appears on fixed-price projects. The contract value is the original amount plus every approved change order. Once the project has started, the amount is changed only by a change order, so there is always a record of why.

    1. Under Change the contract value enter the change in dollars (negative reduces the contract), the reason, and, if you have it, the customer’s signed reference.
    2. Choose Ask for approval. It goes to the project’s owner; if you are the owner, to an administrator. It appears in their Approvals and they must write what they checked when approving.
    3. When approved, the contract value changes. If it is rejected, nothing changes. Until then you can Withdraw it.

    Change orders are never edited or deleted: each stays in the list with its amount, reason, reference, who asked and how it was decided.

    Booking hours and expenses

    Team members book hours on their timesheet and expenses on their expense report; see Time and expenses. Only active projects you are on the team of (or that are open to everyone) are offered. Billable follows the project and task, and a non-billable project or task cannot be billed.

    Cost rates

    People with the cost permission see a Cost rates tab on the Projects page. For each person, enter an effective date and what an hour of their time costs the company (for example, pay plus employer costs and overhead). A new rate applies from its date; earlier work keeps the earlier rate. Entering a rate for a date that already has one replaces it. History lists every rate with who set it.

    Cost rates and project cost are the only pay-related figures in projects, and they are kept apart on purpose: they are absent from every page, export and report for people without the cost permission, and the audit trail records that a rate was set but never the figure. Give the permission only to people who may see staff cost. See Roles and access.

    Alerts and reports

    On the Settings tab, project managers and administrators set the share of the hours budget at which owners are warned (50% to 100%, 90% to start). The daily job tells the owner once when an active project reaches that share and once when it is over its budget; turn it off or have it repeat weekly under Admin → Reminders (see reminders).

    Two reports are in Reports, and can be downloaded as CSV, PDF or Excel:

    • Project status: each project’s hours against budget, percent complete by hours, hours waiting for approval and late milestones; with cost, billed and margin for people who may see cost.
    • Project time: approved hours by person, task, project, week or month, with the billable share. It never shows cost.

    Common mistakes

    • Expecting hours to count at once. Only approved timesheets count as actual; hours waiting for approval are shown separately.
    • Booking to a project you are not on. Ask the owner to add you to the team.
    • Forgetting cost rates. A person without a rate costs nothing, so margin looks better than it is. The project warns you.
    • Changing a fixed-price contract by editing it. Use a change order.
