    Quality and safety

    Quality

    Raise nonconformances, contain and decide what to do with bad product, hold lots, watch SPC charts, and keep calibration and audits on schedule.

    On this page
    • Raise a nonconformance
    • What the NCR statuses mean
    • Work an NCR through
    • SPC control charts
    • Corrective actions tab
    • Calibration
    • Audits
    • My actions
    • Common mistakes

    The Quality page is where problems with product are recorded and dealt with. Anyone who finds something wrong raises a nonconformance (an "NCR"): a record that something does not meet its requirements. Quality staff then contain it, decide what happens to the material, and, for serious problems, fix the cause with a corrective action (see CAPA and 8D). The same page holds SPC control charts, FMEA, calibration plans, audits and your own assigned actions.

    • Raise a nonconformance, record SPC measurements: operators, technicians, buyers and most managers (permission "report quality problems").
    • See everything on the page: quality reviewers, quality managers, plant, maintenance and EHS managers (permission "view quality"). People who can only report see the My actions tab.
    • Contain, disposition, hold lots, close or cancel an NCR; answer SPC signals; set limits; plan calibration and audits; open corrective actions: quality reviewers and quality managers (permission "manage quality"), for their sites.

    Open Quality & safety → Quality. The tabs are Nonconformances, Corrective actions, FMEA and SPC (only if those features are on for your company), Calibration, Audits and My actions.

    Raise a nonconformance

    1. On the Nonconformances tab click Raise a nonconformance.
    2. Choose the Site, where it was found (Internal, Production, Receiving, Supplier or Customer), and the severity.
    3. Optionally find the part and enter the quantity affected.
    4. Describe What is wrong. State facts.
    5. Click Raise. The NCR opens. You can add photos and files at the bottom.
    SeverityUse it when
    MinorA small departure with no effect on safety, fit or customer requirements.
    MajorThe product would not meet its requirements or the customer's.
    CriticalA safety, legal or serious customer risk.

    You can also raise an NCR from a production order that scrapped parts (Raise NCR in its shop-floor record), from an SPC signal, or from a calibration found out of tolerance. Those NCRs are linked to where they came from. A production run can only have one NCR.

    What the NCR statuses mean

    StatusMeaningNext
    OpenRaised but not contained. Major and critical ones are highlighted in red.Record containment.
    ContainedSomething has been done to stop it spreading: quarantine, sort, hold.Decide the disposition.
    DispositionedA decision has been made about the material.Close it, once any needed corrective action is in place.
    ClosedFinished. Held lots are released if the disposition allows.—
    CancelledRaised in error or a duplicate, with a reason.—

    Work an NCR through

    The NCR page has three numbered sections. A quality manager works down them.

    1. Containment. Type what was done to stop it spreading and click Record containment. The NCR becomes Contained.
    2. Hold lots, if the part is lot tracked. Under Lots of … tick the lots and click Put the chosen lots on hold. A held lot cannot be issued or shipped.
    3. Disposition. Choose Scrap, Rework to drawing, Repair (concession), Use as is (concession) or Return to supplier (needs a supplier on the NCR). Optionally enter the cost; for scrap, leaving it blank uses quantity times unit cost. Click Record disposition.
    4. Corrective action. If the NCR is major or critical it needs one before it can close. Either link an open one from the list or click Open a corrective action. For minor ones it is optional.
    5. Click Close nonconformance.

    Accepting a major or critical NCR "as is" or by repair is a concession. It needs a written justification (who accepted it and why), and it must be recorded by someone other than the person who reported it.

    Held lots are released when the NCR closes after use as is, rework or repair. Lots that were scrapped or returned stay held until they leave stock. A supplier NCR also has a link to ask that supplier for a corrective action from the supplier's page; the supplier's answer is recorded on the corrective action. To cancel an NCR, type why it is not a nonconformance and click Cancel.

    SPC control charts

    Statistical process control (SPC) is a way of watching a measurement over time so you act on a trend before parts go out of tolerance. Each characteristic (for example "bore diameter at op 30") has its own chart. The SPC tab lists the characteristics with their status and capability.

    StatusMeaning
    Signal openA rule was broken and no one has answered it yet. Investigate.
    Rule broken, answeredA signal happened and has been answered.
    In controlLimits are set and recent points are within them.
    Initial studyNot enough data for firm limits yet; the chart shows trial limits.
    Not measuredNo recent measurements.
    1. Open the characteristic. The chart shows each point, the centre line, the upper and lower control limits (UCL and LCL) and, for single readings, the tolerance limits.
    2. To record a measurement, enter the values under Record a reading (or Record a subgroup of N, with one box per value in the group), with an optional lot and note, and click Record. The message tells you at once whether a rule was broken. If so, quality has been told: stop and check the process.
    3. Quality managers answer open signals: click Answer, enter the cause (or "none found") and what was done, optionally raise a nonconformance or exclude the point because of an assignable cause, and click Close the signal. Or click Open a CAPA.
    4. Once at least 20 included subgroups exist, a quality manager can click Set limits from all N included subgroups and give a reason.

    The page shows Cpk and Ppk (how comfortably the process fits within tolerance; 1.33 or more is the usual target). Out-of-tolerance values always alert quality. Which rules raise alarms is set for the whole company and can be changed per characteristic, under Rules. Pass/fail counts (defective parts or defects per unit) use p, np, c and u charts instead. A daily alarm summary appears at the top of the SPC tab.

    Corrective actions tab

    This tab lists open corrective actions, with a box to show only yours. Click one to open it. How they work is explained in CAPA and 8D.

    Calibration

    The Calibration tab lists instruments under calibration control, with tolerance, interval, last and next due date.

    StatusMeaning
    CurrentIn date.
    Due soonDue within 14 days.
    OverduePast its due date. Production runs cannot start on it.
    Failed, out of serviceFailed its last calibration. Production runs cannot start on it.
    InactiveNo longer controlled.
    1. To put an instrument under control, click Put an instrument under calibration, choose the site and instrument, the interval in days, the tolerance it must meet, who does it (in house or an external lab) and the first due date.
    2. To record a calibration, open the instrument and fill Record a calibration: date, who, as-found and as-left results, certificate number, readings.

    If the as-found result is out of tolerance, the readings are required and the system raises a nonconformance listing the production runs that used the instrument since the last good calibration. You can attach the certificate to the record.

    Audits

    An audit has a checklist, a lead auditor and an area owner. The lead auditor cannot audit their own area.

    1. A quality manager clicks Plan an audit: site, kind (internal, certification or customer), date, lead auditor, area owner, title, scope and one checklist item per line.
    2. The lead auditor opens the audit and clicks Start audit.
    3. For each item, choose a result (conforming, observation, minor or major nonconformity, not applicable). Anything other than conforming or not applicable needs objective evidence.
    4. Click Issue report with a summary. Every item must have a result first.
    5. Each nonconformity must be answered with a corrective action or an action before the audit can close. A quality manager then clicks Close audit.

    My actions

    The My actions tab lists every action assigned to you from corrective actions, safety incidents, audits, FMEA, continuity plans, store visits and improvements. Overdue ones are marked. Type what was done and click Done.

    Common mistakes

    • Waiting to raise it. Raise the NCR when you find the problem, even if you don't know the cause.
    • Disposition without containment. Contain first so suspect product is not shipped.
    • Closing a major NCR with no corrective action. The system will not let you.
    • Forgetting calibration dates. An overdue gauge stops production runs.

    FMEA and SPC can be switched off separately, and the whole Quality area can be limited to chosen roles. SPC alarm rules apply company-wide; the daily SPC summary is a reminder you can change. See Features and editions and Reminders and retention.
