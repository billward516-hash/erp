    Service companies

    Retainers and subscriptions

    Plans that invoice a fixed amount on schedule, with pause, change, renewal and recurring-revenue reporting.

    On this page
    • The page
    • Create a plan
    • What the daily job does
    • Change a plan
    • Invoices made from a plan
    • When an invoice cannot be made
    • Renewal reminders
    • Recurring revenue
    • Common mistakes

    A retainer or subscription plan is an agreement to bill a customer a fixed amount every month, quarter or year. The daily job makes one invoice per plan per period for you, as a draft or already issued. Open Service companies → Retainers (the page heading reads Retainers and subscriptions).

    The invoices are ordinary customer invoices in Receivables. Work billed by the hour or by milestone is on Time and billing.

    • See plans, their invoices and recurring revenue: billing clerks, finance approvers, bookkeepers, operations managers and executives (permission "view retainers").
    • Create, pause, change, renew and cancel plans, and invoice a period by hand: billing clerks, finance approvers and bookkeepers (permission "manage retainers"). Invoicing by hand also needs receivables authority at the site.
    • Plans belong to a site. People see and manage the plans of the sites they work at.

    The page

    Tiles show Monthly recurring revenue, Annual recurring revenue, Active plans, Paused and Behind schedule or failed. The Plans table lists each plan with its amount, monthly equivalent, status, next invoice date and term. Click a plan to open it.

    Create a plan

    1. Under New plan choose the Customer and, if the fee belongs to a project, the Project. If your company has several sites you can choose the one that invoices.
    2. Write What the customer pays for. It appears on every invoice line.
    3. Enter the Amount, how often it is billed (Billed every month, quarter or year) and the date it Starts.
    4. Optionally set Term ends. A plan without an end date runs until you cancel it. With an end date you can tick Renews by itself at the end of the term and say how many months it renews for, and set the Notice days before the end (default 30).
    5. Leave Charge a part period for the days covered ticked to prorate (below), or untick it to charge a part period in full.
    6. Click Create the plan.

    Periods and proration

    Periods are calendar months, quarters (January, April, July, October) or years. A plan that starts mid-period is charged for the days it covers: a monthly plan of 3,000.00 starting on the 15th of a 31-day month is charged 3,000.00 × 17/31 = 1,645.16 for that first month, then the full amount. The same rule applies to the last period when the term ends or the plan is cancelled part way through a period. With proration off, a part period is charged in full. Invoices are made on the first day a period covers, in advance.

    What the daily job does

    Every day, for each plan, the job (in this order):

    1. Renews plans that renew by themselves and whose term has ended.
    2. Makes the invoice for every period that has started and has none. It makes at most one per plan per period, however many times it runs. A plan that fell behind is caught up twelve periods a day, so a long backlog does not arrive in one pile.
    3. Closes a plan whose term ended without renewal, or whose cancellation date has come, once its last period is invoiced.

    Invoices are drafts by default. An administrator or finance approver can choose Issued automatically (number, PDF and accounting entry) under Billing runs → Settings → Scheduled retainer invoices are. They are made in the name of the person who created the plan, and marked on the plan as made by the daily job.

    Change a plan

    Open the plan. Under Change this plan:

    • Pause (with a reason) and Resume. Periods that start while the plan is paused are skipped and are not billed afterwards. Paused plans do not count in recurring revenue.
    • Change the amount from a date, with a new amount. The date must be the first day of a period that has not been invoiced yet. A period always uses the amount in force on its first day, so past invoices never change.
    • Renew the term for a number of months (default is the plan's renewal term). A plan that ended with its term can be renewed back to life.
    • Notice days and whether the plan renews by itself.
    • Cancel the plan from a date (the last day covered) with a reason. A cancellation cannot cut into a period that is already invoiced: void that invoice first, or cancel from the end of that period. Until the date arrives the plan keeps billing normally.

    Everything you do is kept under History, with who and when.

    Invoices made from a plan

    The plan page lists them: the period, the amount, the invoice and who made it (the daily job or a person). Under Due now are periods with no invoice, with an Invoice now button for people who may.

    If you void a retainer invoice, or remove its line from a draft, that period is released. The daily job does not make it again by itself, so a voided invoice never turns into a loop. Click Invoice now on the plan, or put the period on a billing run (it appears there as a retainer charge).

    A billing run for the customer also lists retainer periods that are due and not yet invoiced, so a customer can get one invoice for hours and retainer together. The period is then stamped so the daily job does not make it a second time.

    When an invoice cannot be made

    If the daily job cannot make a plan's invoice, that plan shows the reason (a red banner on the plan, and Failed in the list), the other plans carry on, and the plan's managers get a reminder, Scheduled retainer invoices that failed. The plan is also on What is late under Retainer invoices behind schedule. Fix the cause and press Invoice now, or the next daily run tries again by itself.

    Renewal reminders

    When a plan with an end date comes inside its own Notice days before the end, its managers get one reminder, Retainers and subscriptions reaching renewal. It says whether the plan renews by itself (so the customer must give notice to stop it) or ends unless renewed. A renewal gets a new end date and so a new reminder next time. An administrator can turn this reminder off under Reminders.

    Recurring revenue

    In Reports, Recurring revenue adds up the plans:

    • Monthly recurring revenue (MRR): each active plan's amount today divided by the months in its period (month 1, quarter 3, year 12). A 9,000.00 quarterly plan counts 3,000.00; a 12,000.00 yearly plan counts 1,000.00.
    • Annual recurring revenue: MRR times 12.
    • New and lost over the last 90 days: plans that started, and the monthly revenue 90 days ago of plans whose cancellation or term end fell in that time.
    • Churn: lost revenue divided by MRR 90 days ago. A pause is not churn; paused plans are simply left out of MRR while they are paused.
    • A table of plans with the next invoice and when each term ends (renewals within 60 days are highlighted), and a chart of MRR at the end of each of the last six months.

    The figures are what the plans schedule. They are not what customers have paid.

    It downloads as CSV, PDF and XLSX like every report.

    Common mistakes

    • "The start date is in the past and nothing was invoiced." The daily job runs once a day; the periods appear on the next run, up to twelve a day.
    • A new amount is refused. It must start on the first day of a period that is not invoiced yet. The message names the next one.
    • Expecting a voided invoice to come back. It does not; see the warning above.

    The feature Retainers and subscriptions is Standard edition and can be turned off on the Features screen, which also stops the daily job making these invoices. See Features and editions and Scheduled jobs, where the step appears as Retainer invoices.
