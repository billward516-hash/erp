    Production

    Shop floor and shift log

    Start and stop jobs at a work center, record good and scrap counts, and hand over to the next shift.

    On this page
    • Open the shop floor
    • Start a job
    • Stop a job and record what you made
    • After you stop
    • The shift log
    • Common mistakes

    This page is for the people doing the work. The Shop floor view shows one work center at a time: its machines, the jobs waiting, and who is running what. You start a job, work, then stop it and record what you made. The Shift log is the written handover to the next shift, built partly from those records.

    • Start and stop your own jobs: operators and production supervisors (permission "execute production"). Your user must be linked to an employee record.
    • See the Shop floor menu item: anyone who can execute or manage production.
    • Start a run for someone else: production supervisors (permission "manage production").
    • Write, hand over and acknowledge a shift log: anyone who can execute or manage work orders or production (technicians, operators, supervisors, managers). Everyone who can view work orders or production can read them.

    Open the shop floor

    Open Production → Shop floor. Choose the Work center from the list. Each entry shows its site, code, name and how many operations are waiting. If you are already running a job, your work center is chosen for you; otherwise the first work center with work waiting is chosen.

    • Machines lists the machines at this work center with their current state (running, idle, available, planned downtime, unplanned downtime, out of service) and the qualifications an operator needs.
    • Waiting here lists operations ready to run: order number, operation, work instruction link, part, quantity waiting and due date. "More to come" means the previous operation has not yet passed units on.
    • Running now lists everyone currently running a job, the machine, and how long they have been going.

    A work instruction link opens the approved version of the instruction in the document viewer. See Document control.

    Start a job

    1. Choose the work center. Check Waiting here for your order and operation.
    2. If you are about to set up rather than run, change Record as from Production run to Setup. Setup time is recorded but setup produces no good units.
    3. If the work center has more than one machine, choose the machine in the box beside the order.
    4. Click Start.

    The Start button only shows when units are waiting. The system refuses to start the job, and says why, if:

    • your user is not linked to an employee record (a supervisor can record the run for you);
    • you already have a job running (stop it first, even if it is at another work center);
    • the machine is already running another job;
    • the machine is in unplanned downtime or out of service until maintenance returns it to service;
    • you do not hold a qualification the machine requires (see Training and performance);
    • the machine is an instrument or gauge that is overdue or failed calibration (see Quality);
    • the operation is finished already, or the order is no longer open.

    When the job starts the order becomes In progress and the machine's state is set to running.

    Stop a job and record what you made

    1. When you are running a job, a box called Your job appears at the top of the page for that work center.
    2. Enter Good: the units that passed. Setup runs have no Good field.
    3. Enter Scrap: the units thrown away. If it is above zero, choose a Scrap reason from the list.
    4. If some of the good units needed rework first, enter that count in Of the good, reworked. It cannot be more than Good.
    5. Add a Note (optional): what happened, adjustments you made.
    6. Click Stop and record.

    You cannot report more units than the operation received. If the numbers don't fit, the message tells you how many units it received and how many are already reported. Check with your supervisor rather than guessing.

    If you have a job running at a different work center, a yellow notice tells you which order and operation to stop first.

    After you stop

    Good units flow to the next operation. When the last operation reports good units, a supervisor receives them into stock from the order page (see Production orders). If scrap was recorded, quality staff can raise a nonconformance for it from the order's shop-floor record (see Quality).

    The shift log

    Open Production → Shift log. It lists the logs for a site, newest first. Pick the site at the top.

    StatusMeaning
    DraftBeing written. The compiled facts are live and keep updating.
    Handed over, not yet readThe writer has handed over. The facts are now frozen. The row is highlighted until the next shift reads it.
    AcknowledgedSomeone on the next shift read it and took over. Their name and time are on the log.

    The list also shows area, who wrote it, open items (with a red tag for open safety items), machines down, and who read it.

    Write up your shift

    1. At the top of the Shift log page, optionally type an Area. Leave it blank for the whole site.
    2. Click Write up this shift. A new log opens, covering the last eight hours. It is named 1st, 2nd or 3rd by the site's local hour (shifts ending 06:00 to 14:00 are "1st", to 22:00 "2nd", otherwise "3rd").
    3. Read the facts the system compiled: machines down, urgent work orders open, what was made and scrapped per work center, orders that are behind, nonconformances, and open SPC signals. You do not have to retype these.
    4. Under Safety, write anything the next shift must know: spills, bypassed guards, lockouts left in place. Safety incidents reported this shift are listed under it.
    5. Under Open items, pick a category (safety, equipment, quality, production, people, other), write what the next shift should follow up, and click Add.
    6. Write how the shift went under Notes and click Save notes.
    7. Click Hand over to the next shift.

    Items still open from the previous log are carried into your new log automatically, marked "carried from". When one is dealt with, type how in How it was resolved and click Resolved. Unresolved items keep carrying forward until someone resolves them.

    Starting a log when you already have a draft for the same site and area opens that draft rather than a second one. The shift log covers at most 24 hours.

    Read a handover

    1. Open the log marked Handed over, not yet read.
    2. Read the safety box, open items and facts.
    3. Optionally type a note under Note on taking over.
    4. Click I have read this and taken over.

    The person who wrote a log cannot acknowledge it; the incoming shift does. Maintenance and production managers at the site are notified when a log is handed over, with the number of open items and machines down.

    Common mistakes

    • Leaving a job running at the end of the shift. It keeps counting time, and you cannot start another. Stop it and record the counts first.
    • Counting scrap as good. Always enter scrap with a reason; quality uses the reasons to find causes.
    • Writing the shift log after you leave. The facts freeze when you hand over, and the handover is for the people arriving. Write it before you go.

    Scrap reasons, work centers and machine qualification requirements are set up by managers and administrators. Work centers and products are explained in Production orders. The shift log relies on the Work orders feature; see Features and editions. Site hours decide the shift names; see Sites and people.
