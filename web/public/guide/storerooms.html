    Maintenance

    Storerooms

    See what parts you have, receive and adjust stock, count it, and track lots and serial numbers.

    On this page
    • Stock on hand
    • Receive parts
    • Issue parts
    • Adjust a balance
    • Reorder levels
    • Units of measure
    • Counting stock
    • Lots and serial numbers
    • Common mistakes

    Storerooms hold your spare parts. Every change to a quantity, whether a receipt, an issue to a work order, a return, a transfer, an adjustment or a count, is written to a ledger, so you can always see how a balance came about. This page shows stock by storeroom and is where you receive, adjust, count and track lots.

    Who can do this: anyone with stock access (technicians, buyers, managers) can view stock, the ledger, lots and kits. Maintenance managers and others with "manage inventory" can receive parts, post adjustments, set reorder levels, run counts, put lots on hold and map barcodes. Issuing parts to a work order only needs the right to work on that job. Changing a part's tracking or units needs "manage parts" (maintenance managers and buyers).

    Open Maintenance → Storerooms. There are four tabs: Stock, Lots, Kits and Cycle counts. For barcode work on a tablet see Scan station.

    Stock on hand

    Four counters sit at the top: Stocked lines, At or below reorder point, Out of stock and Stock value (at average cost). Below, search by part number or description, or tick Only at or below reorder point. A row shows storeroom, part, bin, on-hand quantity, reorder level and last movement, with an Out or Reorder badge where needed.

    See how a balance came about

    1. Click a stock row. A panel opens for that part at that storeroom.
    2. Read the movements: when, type, quantity (+ or -), balance after, reference (work order, packing slip or reason) and who did it.

    Receive parts

    Use this for stock arriving without going through a purchase order. If you have a purchase order, receive against it so the order stays accurate; see Purchasing or the scan station.

    1. On the Stock tab, scroll to Receive parts.
    2. Choose the Storeroom and the Part.
    3. Enter the Quantity. Optionally add Unit cost (USD) and a Packing slip / PO reference.
    4. If the part is tracked, enter the lot (with an expiry date if it has one) or list the serial numbers, one per line. The page shows "N of M serial numbers" so you can check the count.
    5. Click Receive. A green message gives the new balance.

    You can split a delivery across lots with Split into another lot. The unit cost feeds the part's average cost.

    Issue parts

    Parts leave a storeroom in one of three ways:

    • A technician records Record use on a work order and chooses the storeroom. See Work orders: record parts.
    • A storeroom clerk issues a prepared kit to the job. See Kits. The Kits tab lists open kits needed by date, with any shortage flagged.
    • The scan station issues by scanning to a work order or a job reference.

    Stock cannot go below zero. If a tracked part has several lots, the first to expire is used unless someone chooses another; lots on hold or past expiry are never issued.

    Adjust a balance

    1. Click the stock row to open the part panel.
    2. Under Adjust, enter Change (+ or -) and a Reason of at least five characters, such as "damaged in storage".
    3. For a tracked part, enter the lot or serial. It is required when adding, and optional when removing (blank means the first to expire).
    4. Click Post adjustment.

    For finding a difference on the shelf, a count is better than an adjustment: it records what you found and posts the difference for you.

    Reorder levels

    1. Open the part panel.
    2. Under Reorder settings, set the Reorder point and Reorder quantity.
    3. Click Save.

    A part at or below its reorder point is flagged Reorder and counted in the top counters. Buyers then raise a purchase request; see Purchasing.

    Units of measure

    A part is stocked and counted in one unit, shown in the Units section of the part panel. If it is bought in packs, boxes, cases or drums, someone with part-management rights can add a unit ("1 BOX = 100 EA"), so purchasing and receiving convert for you. A unit that a supplier is priced in cannot be removed.

    Counting stock

    Open the Cycle counts tab. It lists counts with storeroom, status, how many lines are counted, and when opened and posted.

    1. Choose a Storeroom to count… and click Start a count. A count sheet opens listing every part there, with bin and the expected quantity as of that moment.
    2. Count the shelf. Enter each Counted quantity. Click Save counts to keep your progress.
    3. If you find a tracked lot that is not on the sheet, use Found a lot that is not on the sheet to add it.
    4. When every line is counted, click Post count. A green message lists the differences, or "No variances."

    If stock moved while you counted, the page stops and says so. Click Post against current quantities to accept the moves and post the differences against today's numbers.

    Count statusMeaning
    OpenStill being counted. Counts can be saved and continued.
    PostedDifferences have been written to the ledger. The sheet is read-only.

    A count started on the scan station is blind: expected quantities are hidden until it is posted, so the counter is not nudged toward the book figure.

    Lots and serial numbers

    A part can be tracked by lot (a batch, often with an expiry date) or by serial number (one unit each). The Lots tab lists tracked stock. Search by lot, serial, supplier lot or part, or use the filter: In stock, Expired or expiring within 30 days, or On hold.

    Lot statusMeaning
    AvailableCan be issued or shipped.
    ExpiredPast its expiry date. Never issued or shipped.
    On holdBlocked, with a reason. Never issued or shipped.
    ScrappedWritten off.

    Switch tracking on for a part

    1. Open the part panel from the Stock tab.
    2. Under Lot and serial tracking, choose By lot or By serial number. Optionally set a Shelf life in days, which fills in expiry dates on receipt.
    3. If stock is already on hand, enter a lot number (and expiry) for each storeroom's quantity so it can be put into a lot now.
    4. Click Save.

    To track by serial number, count the stock out to zero first (or track by lot). The page warns you if there is stock on hand.

    A lot's page

    Click a lot to see where it is, what it was made from, where it went (production orders, customer shipments, work orders) and every movement. Print label makes a barcode label. Find every customer who received it follows the lot through production to a list of customers and shipments, which is what you need in a recall.

    To block a lot, type a reason and click Put on hold. Click Release the hold to free it.

    Common mistakes

    • Adjusting stock to fix a count difference. Run a count instead, so the record shows what was found.
    • Receiving a tracked part without a lot or serial. The system will not accept it.
    • Forgetting to set reorder points. Without them nothing is flagged.
    • Leaving a count open for days while stock moves. Count a small area, post it promptly.

    Administrators: storerooms (stock locations) and parts are set up by import or by your implementation team; see Importing data and Sites and people. To give someone count and receive rights, give them a role with "manage inventory"; see Roles and access.
