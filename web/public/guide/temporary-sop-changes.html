    Quality and safety

    Temporary SOP changes

    Change a procedure for a stated period, with approval, a banner for readers, and a timer that forces a decision when it runs out.

    On this page
    • Where to find it
    • Start a temporary change
    • What the statuses mean
    • What readers see
    • Expiry timers and alerts
    • Extend, revert or make permanent
    • Cancel
    • Common mistakes

    Sometimes a procedure has to be different for a while: a part is out of stock so you use a substitute, a machine is down so you use a workaround, a customer allows a deviation. A temporary SOP change records exactly what is different, why, and until when, without rewriting the procedure. While it is in force, everyone who opens that procedure sees a banner with the change and its end date. When the date arrives, the change cannot quietly carry on: it must be reverted, extended or made permanent.

    • Start a temporary change and manage your own: anyone who can request changes, such as operators, technicians, buyers and managers. The person who created it, or its owner, can submit, extend, revert, cancel or make it permanent.
    • Approve, and manage anyone's: change coordinators (maintenance managers and plant managers; permission "manage changes"). Nobody can approve their own change.
    • See them: anyone who can view changes.

    Where to find it

    Open Quality & safety → Changes and click the Temporary SOP changes tab. The tiles show how many are in force, how many end within 14 days, and how many are past their end date. Tick Only changes in force or waiting to start to hide finished ones. The Time left column is coloured:

    ColourTime leftMeaning
    GreenMore than 14 daysNo action needed yet.
    Amber4 to 14 daysStart deciding what happens at the end.
    Red3 days or fewerDecide now.
    Red, "days over"The end date has passedExpired. The procedure still carries the change. Act at once.

    Start a temporary change

    1. Click New temporary change.
    2. Choose the Site and the Procedure. The list shows controlled SOPs, work instructions, procedures, methods, repair procedures, manuals and troubleshooting guides that have an approved revision.
    3. Set Starts (today by default) and Ends. The end cannot be before the start or in the past.
    4. Write a short Title, then What is different while it is in force (at least ten characters) and Why it is needed.
    5. Click Save draft.
    6. On the change page, under Next step, choose an Approver (a change coordinator at the site, other than you) and click Submit for approval.

    The whole period, from start to end, can't be longer than the company's ceiling (90 days unless your administrator changed it). For anything longer, change the procedure itself.

    What the statuses mean

    StatusMeaningWhat happens next
    DraftBeing written.Submit it for approval, or cancel.
    Waiting for approvalThe approver has been asked.On approval it starts (see below). Not approved sends it back to you.
    Not approvedThe approver refused it. You are told.Revise and submit again, or cancel.
    Approved, not startedApproved, but the start date is still ahead.It goes into force on the start date.
    In forceLive. The banner is shown on the procedure.It runs to its end date unless reverted, extended or made permanent.
    Expired: needs a decisionThe end date passed and nobody has resolved it. The banner stays up, marked as past its end date.Revert, extend or make permanent.
    Becoming permanentA normal change has been opened to put the wording into the procedure.The banner and timer keep running until that change is approved.
    RevertedThe procedure is back to its approved form.Finished.
    Made permanentThe change for it was approved.Finished.
    CancelledDropped before it began.Finished.

    What readers see

    While a change is in force (or expired and unresolved), a banner appears at the top of the procedure, including a work instruction written in the app. It reads Temporary change in force with the change number, what is different, the end date and the days left. Past the end date it reads Temporary change past its end date. The banner links to the details. Treat it as part of the procedure.

    Expiry timers and alerts

    A daily check runs for every change that is in force. The change's owner is alerted at 14, 7, 3, 1 and 0 days before the end date. The approver is also alerted at 3, 1 and 0 days. Each alert is sent once. If the end date passes and the change is not resolved:

    • the status changes to Expired: needs a decision;
    • the owner is alerted every day;
    • from the seventh day, change coordinators at the site are alerted as well (escalation), every day, until it is resolved.

    There is also a weekly reminder for temporary changes ending soon, which your administrator can switch off or adjust. It is separate from the daily alerts above.

    Extend, revert or make permanent

    When the end is near, or after it has passed, you have three ways to resolve the change. Use the Next step card on the change page.

    Extend

    1. Under Extend, choose a New end date later than the current one.
    2. Give a Reason (at least five characters) and choose an approver.
    3. Click Request extension.

    Each extension needs its own reason and fresh approval. One extension can add at most the company's ceiling in days, counted from the later of the current end date or today. Only one request can wait at a time. If the approver refuses, you are told to revert or make it permanent. A change extended twice or more shows a warning: if it is still needed, make it permanent.

    Revert

    1. Put the procedure back to its normal form and tell the people who used the change.
    2. Type a note confirming that (at least five characters).
    3. Click Revert: procedure is back to normal. The banner disappears.

    Make permanent

    1. Click Make it permanent (opens a change).
    2. A normal change of the type "procedure" is created, linked to the procedure, with the temporary wording as its description. Take it through the usual steps in Changes and notices, and revise the document through Document control.

    The banner and the timer keep running until that change is approved. Then the temporary change is marked Made permanent. If the change is rejected or cancelled, the temporary change returns to its previous state.

    Cancel

    A draft, a change waiting for approval, or a change that was not approved can be cancelled with a reason. Once a change has been approved, use Revert instead.

    Common mistakes

    • Letting it expire. The procedure still carries the change, you get an alert every day, and coordinators are told after a week. Decide before the end date.
    • Extending again and again. A change extended more than once is probably permanent. Make it permanent.
    • Reverting only in your head. The revert needs a note confirming the procedure is back to normal and people are told.
    • Choosing yourself as approver. The system refuses.

    The ceiling (default 90 days, from 1 to 365) is set by an administrator on the Document control page under Document settings as "Longest temporary SOP change". It limits both the whole period and each extension. The weekly "Temporary changes ending" reminder, with its lead time, is in the reminders catalogue. Approvers must hold the manage-changes permission at the site. See Reminders and retention and Sites and people.
