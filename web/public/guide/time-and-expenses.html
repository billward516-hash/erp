    My day

    Time and expenses

    Fill in and submit your weekly timesheet, claim business expenses with receipts, and understand the approvals, rules and statuses behind both.

    On this page
    • Timesheets
    • Expenses
    • Common mistakes

    Two things in My day touch your pay: Time (your weekly timesheet) and Expenses (money you spent for the company and want back). This page covers both: how to fill them in, who approves them, and what each status means.

    Timesheets

    A timesheet covers one week, Monday to Sunday. Hours you book to work orders appear on it by themselves. You add everything else (training, meetings, leave) and then submit the week for approval.

    The My time tab is for anyone linked to an employee record. If your user is not linked to one, the page says so and there is no timesheet for you; ask your administrator. The Team tab is for people who approve time (maintenance managers and plant managers, for instance) or see all timesheets (payroll, HR). Payroll export is for payroll administrators.

    Fill in and submit your week

    1. Open My day → Time. The current week is shown. Use the arrows to go to earlier weeks; you cannot go past the current one. This week brings you back.
    2. Check the day boxes and the table. Rows marked from work order are hours you or a colleague recorded on a work order; they are read-only here. To correct them, change the work order.
    3. To add other time, pick the Day, the Type (for example Regular hours, Paid time off, Holiday, Sick leave, Unpaid leave), the Hours (in quarter-hours) and an optional note such as “training”. Choose Add time.
    4. Use Remove to delete a line you added by mistake.
    5. Read any yellow warnings. You are warned when worked hours in the week go over 40, or a single day shows more than 12 hours. The system does not calculate overtime; your approver and your payroll provider handle that.
    6. Choose Submit week for approval. The button needs at least some hours on the sheet.

    Once submitted, the week's work-order hours are locked. If something changes, ask your supervisor to send the sheet back.

    Timesheet statuses

    StatusMeaningWhat you can do
    Not startedYou have opened no sheet for the week yet.Open it and add time.
    DraftYou are still filling it in.Add or remove time; submit.
    Waiting for approvalSubmitted. Hours are locked.Wait; ask your supervisor to send it back if it is wrong.

    Approving time

    1. Open Time → Team. Choose the week (it starts on last week). The summary shows how many are waiting, approved or not submitted. Rows waiting for approval are highlighted.
    2. Click a person. Their sheet opens with the same day boxes, table and warnings.
    3. Choose Approve [hours] h, or write a note and choose Send back. The note is required to send back and tells the employee what to fix.
    4. To change an approved sheet that has not yet gone to payroll, write a reason and choose Reopen.

    You can never approve your own timesheet. If you entered a sheet on someone else's behalf, someone else must approve it. A sheet already sent to payroll cannot be reopened here.

    Payroll export

    Payroll administrators use Time → Payroll export to create a file of approved hours:

    1. Choose the From week and To week, and a Layout: one line per person, week and earning code, or per person, day and earning code.
    2. Read the preview. Timesheets that are not approved are listed as not ready and stay out of the file; they can go into a later one.
    3. Choose Create payroll file, then Download it from Payroll files. Exported weeks are locked.
    4. Under Earning codes, set the code your payroll provider expects for each type of hours.

    Pay rates, overtime, taxes and payments stay in your payroll provider.

    A reminder tells people who have not submitted last week's timesheet, and tells approvers when sheets are waiting. See Administrator brief: reminders and retention. Approvers are anyone who holds the approve-time permission at the site; see Roles and access.

    Expenses

    An expense report is a bundle of expenses (lines) for one trip or purpose, with receipts. You submit it, one or more people approve it, and then finance records the reimbursement.

    Everyone can claim expenses. All reports is for finance (bookkeepers, finance approvers, accounts payable). Approval rules is for those who manage expenses.

    Claim an expense

    1. Open My day → Expenses. Under New expense report type a title, such as “Customer visit, Chicago”, and the business purpose, then choose Start report.
    2. Under Add an expense, set the date, pick the Category, and enter the amount. For Mileage (personal vehicle) enter the miles instead; the system multiplies by the company's rate. Add a merchant and a description. For business meals, also say who attended and why.
    3. Choose Add expense. Repeat for each expense.
    4. Attach a receipt to each line with Attach (a photo or a PDF, up to 10 MB). Replace swaps it.
    5. Read the notes beside each line (see below) and fix or explain them. The banner reads Ready to submit when all is in order.
    6. Check the Approval route, then choose Submit for approval.

    The categories start as: Airfare, Lodging, Meals while traveling, Business meals with guests, Taxi/rideshare/rental car, Mileage, Parking and tolls, Office and shop supplies, Training and conferences, Phone and internet, Tools and small equipment, and Other (explain). Your company may have changed them.

    Fix and Explain flags

    Each line is checked against the company's expense rules (see Expense rules). A flag marked Fix blocks submission; Explain lets you submit if you type an explanation (at least five characters) in the line's Explanation box.

    FlagKindWhat to do
    The date is in the future; no category; no amount or miles; trip not described; attendees missing; “Other” without a description; mileage rate not setFixCorrect the line. If the mileage rate is not set, finance must set it before mileage can be claimed.
    Receipt required over the limitExplainAttach the receipt, or explain why there is none.
    Over the limit per expense or per day for the categoryExplainExplain the reason.
    Older than the claim deadlineExplainExplain the delay.
    Looks like a duplicate (same date, amount and merchant, in this report or another)ExplainDelete the duplicate, or explain why both are real.

    How approval works

    The route depends on site, department and amount. In the starter setup, the head of your department signs every report; reports of 500 and over also need finance review, and 5,000 and over need executive approval. If you are a department head claiming your own expenses, the step goes to the role instead. Stages are signed in order. Approvers decide on the report page (Approve or Reject, with a comment required to reject) or from Approvals.

    In a very small company where nobody else could sign, the owner may approve their own report; the record shows it was self-approved.

    Recording the reimbursement

    Finance opens an approved report and, under Record the reimbursement, sets the date, the method (With payroll, Direct deposit, Check or Other) and a reference, then chooses Mark reimbursed. The date cannot be in the future, and you cannot record the payment of your own report.

    Setting the rules (managers)

    The Approval rules tab lists each step with its stage, who signs it (a role or the department head), the amount it starts at, and the site and department it applies to. Add a rule with Add rule, or switch one off with Turn off. Under Department heads choose who heads each department. Limits, receipt thresholds, the mileage rate and the claim deadline are edited under Policies → Expense rules.

    Expense categories are tied to accounts in the books, so approved expenses land in the right place. See Administrator brief: accounting connection. If you submit and the system says no approval rule matches, finance needs to add one.

    Common mistakes

    • Forgetting to submit. A draft timesheet or report is invisible to approvers. Drafts and sheets that are late trigger reminders.
    • Putting work-order time on the sheet twice. Hours from work orders arrive automatically; add only other time.
    • Submitting without receipts. You will be asked to explain each missing one.
    • Claiming mileage before a rate exists. It is blocked until the company sets one.
