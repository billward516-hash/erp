    Maintenance

    Work orders

    Turn a problem into a job, assign it, record labor and parts, then verify and close it.

    A work order is one job on one asset: what is wrong or what must be done, who does it, which parts and hours it used, and who checked it afterward. Every maintenance job in the system, whether a breakdown, a scheduled preventive task or a contractor visit, follows the same life cycle.

    • Request a job: operators, technicians, store managers and most managers (permission "request work orders").
    • Create, assign, schedule, cancel, close, dispatch contractors: maintenance managers, facilities managers, district managers (permission "manage work orders"). A service dispatcher also holds it.
    • Start work, add labor and parts, mark complete: maintenance technicians and managers (permission "execute work orders").
    • Verify: maintenance managers, facilities managers, district managers, store managers (permission "verify work orders").

    From a request to a work order

    Planners and managers can also start a job directly: click New work order at the top of the list, choose the asset, give it a title, type, priority and optional due date, and click Create work order. The rest of this section covers the usual route, through a request.

    Most jobs begin as a request. Anyone can send one from an asset page (Assets: report a problem), and the site's public request link, if your company uses one, adds requests from outside. Requests wait in an inbox for a planner.

    1. Open Maintenance → Work orders and click Requests. (You only see this button if you can manage work orders.)
    2. Read the request: when it arrived, the site and asset, the problem, who sent it, and the severity. Down and Safety flags show in red. Critical and high requests are highlighted.
    3. Click Create work order. A short form appears with a title (taken from the problem) and a priority set from the severity.
    4. Adjust the title and priority (1 Emergency, 2 High, 3 Normal, 4 Low) and click Create. The new work order opens.

    If the request said the machine was down, the machine is put into unplanned downtime counted from when it was reported (unless its state has changed since). If you cannot act on a request, click Decline, give a reason (duplicate, not a maintenance issue) and click Decline request.

    Preventive maintenance creates its own work orders. See Preventive maintenance. Field service visits get one automatically; see Field service.

    The work order list

    The list has four views: Open, Assigned to me (if you are linked to an employee record), All and Requests. Each row shows the number, site, asset, title, priority, type, status, contractor, service-level pills and due date. Overdue jobs are marked in red.

    Open a work order and do the work

    Click any row to open the work order. The Next step card shows the one action available to you now, plus the description and any safety notes in a yellow box.

    1. Click Start work. The system checks that everyone assigned still holds the qualifications the machine requires, and refuses if not.
    2. Record your time with Labor (below) and parts with Parts (below) as you go.
    3. When done, fill in the form: Work done and Findings. For corrective (breakdown) jobs, What fixed it? (required) must be filled in.
    4. Click Mark work complete. At least one labor entry must exist first (or a contractor's completion).

    Assign people

    1. On the work order, find the People card.
    2. In Assign a technician…, pick an active employee at the site and click Assign.

    The system refuses if the person lacks a qualification the machine requires. You can assign until the work is completed. The new assignee is notified.

    Record labor

    1. While the job is In progress or On hold, use the row under Labor.
    2. Pick the Work date and enter Hours (in quarter-hours, up to 24).
    3. Click Add my hours.

    Hours are added under your name. You must be linked to an employee record to see the form. The card total shows hours and labor cost.

    Record parts

    1. While the job is In progress, use the form under Parts.
    2. Choose a part. Parts planned for this job are listed first with a star.
    3. If your site stocks the part, choose the storeroom in Taken from…. Storerooms with nothing on hand are greyed out.
    4. Enter the quantity and click Record use.

    Stocked parts come out of that storeroom's balance. A part that is not stocked at this site is recorded without a stock movement. To put unused parts back, click Return on the line, enter how many, and confirm. You can return until the job is closed.

    Kits and repair templates

    The Kit card helps the storeroom prepare parts in advance. A planner can apply a repair template (a stock list of parts and hours for a known job), then click Build kit from planned parts to reserve the stock. The storeroom stages the kit; the technician clicks Issue kit to this job, and later Return unused and close kit. If a kit is still reserved or staged when the job is completed or cancelled, the reservation is released automatically.

    Parts you do not have

    If planned parts exceed what has been used, a Purchasing card offers Request parts not in stock (for people who may raise purchase requests). It shows each request and order, expected dates and what has arrived. See Purchasing.

    Send a job to a contractor

    1. On an open work order, click Send to a contractor in the Contractor card.
    2. Optionally type a Trade to filter, choose the contractor, and set a Not to exceed ($) limit and a note.
    3. Click Dispatch.
    4. As the contractor responds, record Accepted, Declined, On site or Completed. Completing requires a note of what the contractor did.

    Contractors with a lapsed insurance certificate cannot be chosen. The contractor is emailed if an address is on file. Invoices against the job are totalled against the limit, and the card warns if you are over it. For contractor work, the buttons to start and complete the job yourself are hidden.

    Verify, reopen and close

    Verification is the return-to-service check. It must be done by someone other than the person who completed the work; the system refuses otherwise.

    1. Open a Completed work order. Check the work.
    2. If it is good, click Verify and return to service. If the machine was in downtime, it goes back to Available. If the job came from a PM plan, the plan moves to its next due date.
    3. If it is not good, click Reopen. The job returns to In progress with a note "Verification failed".
    4. A planner then clicks Close on the Verified job.

    Anyone without verify permission sees "Waiting for someone else to verify and return the asset to service."

    If the machine is under equipment qualification control and needs requalification (finishing work on such a machine usually makes it need it), the system refuses to return it to service and says why. Click Verify, keep out of service to verify the work without returning the machine, then return it once its requalification protocol is approved.

    Service levels

    The coloured pills in the title area, Response and Completion, compare the job to the targets set for its priority: met, on track, at risk or missed. Starting work records the response.

    Working with a poor connection

    If you lose connection, the work order page shows the last copy saved on your device, and any changes you make are queued and shown as "Waiting to be sent". Open see all to review them. A refused change shows the reason. See Offline and tablets.

    Common mistakes

    • Trying to verify your own work. Ask a colleague with verify permission.
    • Marking a breakdown complete with no remedy or no hours. Both are required.
    • Forgetting to pick the storeroom when recording a stocked part. The Record use button stays disabled until you do.
    • Leaving a job on hold. On-hold and overdue jobs appear in What is late.

    Administrators: who may request, execute, verify and manage is set by roles; see Roles and access and the permissions table. Service-level targets, reminders for overdue work and retention are covered in Reminders and retention. Qualification checks on assignment depend on the training records you maintain.
